### Five-act and nested examples (Yorke)

John Yorke, *Into the Woods*, maps *Macbeth* onto five stages: witches' prophecy and the decision to murder Duncan; Macbeth becomes king; Macduff defects; Lady Macbeth goes mad and Macbeth is abandoned (worst point); final battle and Macbeth killed.

- Yorke's nested *Raiders* opening: Indiana Jones seeks a golden idol; desire is retrieval; midpoint is taking the idol from the inner temple; crisis is Belloq's surprise; climax is Belloq stealing the idol; resolution is Indy's escape with his life.

| Film | Plot Point I | Plot Point II |
|---|---|---|
| Chinatown | The real Mrs. Mulwray appears (threatening defamation lawsuit) | Glasses discovered in the garden pool |
| Three Days of the Condor | Returning with lunch to find all colleagues slaughtered | Shifts from victim to counter-attacker: kidnaps Robertson |
| Rocky | Offered the title match against Apollo (minute 20) | Runs up the museum steps (minute 88) |
| Network | Dunaway persuades the network to put Finch back on the air | The "Takeover Speech" |
| An Unmarried Woman | Husband confesses he loves someone else (minute 25) | The pair leave the social gathering together (minute 85) |
| Nashville | The ensemble departs the airport | Murphy convinces Garfield to let Blakley perform |
| Silver Streak | A corpse drops outside the window during a sleeper-car tryst | — |

The first ten pages of *Chinatown*: Curly sobs while looking at photographs of his wife's infidelity; Gittes reaches for a cheaper whiskey rather than the top-shelf bottle to offer him, declaring that getting away with murder requires being rich (foreshadowing the ending); frosted glass reading "Private Investigator" on the door establishes the dramatic situation; on page five the impostor Mrs. Mulwray announces she believes her husband is seeing another woman, launching the dramatic premise; by page eight at City Hall, dialogue establishes Los Angeles as a city perched on a desert, giving the backdrop; on page ten a farmer drives sheep inside demanding who paid for this stunt, restating the dramatic question. The screenplay omits descriptions of Gittes's physical appearance, relying exclusively on telling actions (the white linen suit, dabbing sweat).

## III. BS2 Beat-by-Beat Case: *Miss Congeniality*

Opening childhood brawl beating up a boy leads to ostracization → Theme Stated: "I do not need femininity" → Catalyst: murder threat at the pageant → Debate: "Can she pull it off?" (mentor makeover) → Miniskirt transformation followed by a face-plant stumble → Fun and Games (tackling a suspect during a water-glass performance) → B Story centered on female bonding among contestants → All Is Lost: ordered off the case by superiors, stripped of FBI badge (Whiff of Death), mentor leaves behind a dress → Break into Three: the girls embrace her → On-stage self-defense demonstration with Benjamin (tough yet glamorous) → Final Image: surrounded by women, receiving the "Miss Congeniality" award.

*Memento*: A gimmick that failed commercially (Snyder's assessment, citing it as proof that high concept does not equal a gimmick).

## IV. Nine Beats Case: *Jojo Rabbit*

- Basic Exposition: Ordered to kill a rabbit → Refuses → Branded "Jojo Rabbit" (Inciting Event).
- Argument: Imaginary Hitler encourages him to "be the rabbit" → Tosses grenade and wounds himself = "long road ahead" (Weak first commitment).
- Preliminary Progress: Sees executed traitors; discovers Elsa; confrontation → mocking → rational inquiries.
- Raising the Stakes: Fights with mother, grows increasingly attached to Elsa.
- Midpoint: Butterflies fluttering in his stomach = love (Second commitment).
- Commitment: Gestapo inspection, Elsa poses as Inge, the captain confirms her cover.
- Crisis: Mother hanged in the square → attacks Elsa → rejects Hitler → pours his heart out for the first time.
- Confrontation: Allied forces breach the city, the captain spits on him to spare his life.
- Resolution: Confesses love to Elsa, leads her outside onto the street to dance.

The three acts of *Winter's Bone*: Act I reveals the father put up the house as bail collateral, the protagonist resolves to track him down, met by stonewalls everywhere; Act II sees her persist despite threats, Teardrop steps in, the Midpoint hits when the father skips court giving a one-week deadline, she confronts Thump Milton head-on, gets beaten, Teardrop rescues her; Act III brings women leading her to the submerged corpse to sever its hands with a chainsaw, she presents proof to the sheriff, the family home is saved, Teardrop tracks down the culprits and heads into a fatal vendetta. Distinctive trait: Rhythmic causality—efforts often yield no immediate results, breakthroughs arrive unprompted, intensifying isolation and audience empathy.

## V. McKee's Proportions and Design Principles

- Scene Count: 40–60 scenes, averaging 2.5 minutes; one-minute scenes balanced by four-minute scenes; scenes compress and accelerate when charging toward an act climax **to earn the right to pause**; major turning-point scenes themselves unspool slowly, drawn-out and tense ("A climax is not a brief burst, but profound change").
- Act Proportions: Act I ≈ 25% (climax around minutes 20–30); final act shortest at ≤ 20 minutes; the Act II swamp is navigated through subplots (one-act A, two-act B, three-act C generating five reversals) or expanding act count (Mid-Act Climax).
- Transition Principle: The third element between two consecutive scenes = commonality or direct opposition—character traits (mischievous child → childish adult), actions (foreplay → afterglow), objects (greenhouse → jungle), a snippet of dialogue, quality of light (dawn shadows → sunset glow), sounds (ocean waves → snoring), ideas (blank canvas → dying elder).
- Pacing: Act I tension → Act II opens with slackness → re-accelerates beyond the previous act, akin to a distance runner varying stride; "tightening the screw slowly wears the audience out."
- Four Levels of Negative Progression (Constructing the Wall of Negativity): Positive Value → Contrary Value (Unfairness) → Contradictory Value (Injustice) → Negation of the Negation (Tyranny / "Seemingly positive negativity"). Typically Act I drops to the contrary, subsequent acts to the contradictory, and the final act plunges to the negation of the negation.
- Fourfold Effect of a Turning Point: Surprise → Increased Curiosity → Insight → New Direction; the question "why" propels the audience backward into preceding scenes, where answers were quietly planted in advance. Turning points operate through only two devices: **Action or Revelation**; powerful revelations hold back crucial backstory until the moment of crisis.
- Setup and Payoff: A setup carries one meaning on first viewing, revealing secondary, tertiary, and quaternary layers upon reflection (Cross's "for my daughter"); a payoff in turn functions as a new setup; narrative logic reconstructs retroactively.
- Diminishing Emotional Returns: Three consecutive tragic scenes → tears → sobbing → laughter; only two primary emotions exist (pleasure and pain), and tone can never substitute for emotion.
- Crisis Placement Variations: Commonly shares the same scene at the final threshold (*Thelma & Louise* freeze-frame slow-motion); the crisis turns the penultimate act, leaving the entire final act for climactic action (*Casablanca*: Rick sends Ilsa off with Laszlo → 15-minute airport escape plan); very rarely, the crisis immediately follows the Inciting Incident, turning the whole film into one extended climactic movement (James Bond accepts the mission; *Leaving Las Vegas*: resolving to drink himself to death).

## VI. Lu Jun: Catalog of Openings and Endings

### Eight Opening Methods
| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| Conflict Method | *A Sunny Morning*: lovers cross paths in a park, squabble, exchange romantic memoirs, and recognize each other; *Rainy Night*: "Grandpa, I know who you are waiting for, but let me tell you the village chief will never show up!" |
| Arrangement Method | *Another Way Out*: bluntly complaining about being treated as spouses, resolving to commit infidelity once each; *Happy Song*: offstage chorus sings "Today on New Year's Eve the honor roll is posted, let us see who is the richest" while Li Xiaoxiao stages a scene of the God of Wealth crying poverty, then celebrates the New Year all over again |
| Atmosphere Method | *Benevolent Heart*: trivial bickering while a life hangs in the balance, two tourists betting ten against a hundred on the cliff-jumper's age; *Peace of Mind*: exalting the joys of a fish peddler's yoke, boasting "even if the Emperor wished to trade places I would have to consider it" |
| Surprise Method | *Silkworm Village Maiden*: the troupe's star hands in a transfer request just as a blind girl arrives to audition |
| Cross-Purposes Method | *Melon Harvest Season*: Ah Fu, arriving to sponge off the wealthy, announces "just send word and I will pick up my share myself" |
| Stunner Method | *Flower-Growing Family*: on the opening day of the cultural hall, the patriarch decrees every evening must be spent weaving three flower baskets |
| Self-Introduction Method | Brecht's *The Exception and the Rule*: chorus and merchant soliloquy; *The Car*: narrator opens with "Across thirty-two minutes, ninety-seven pedestrians bypassed this vehicle" |
| Driven to the Brink Method | *Riding the Fence*: Ah Mao, having stolen a breeding rabbit, sits astride the wall while Lanhua puts him on trial as the "thief" |

### Eight Ending Methods
| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| Thunderclap Ending | *The Twelve-Pound Look*: the lady asks, "Were they very expensive?" "What?" "Those typewriters."; *The Acting Inspector*: from next door, "I am the inspector, I have stayed beside your room for three nights"; *The Confession*: "He would rather confess," Baldwin sinks into his chair repeating "confess"; *Selling Bayberries*: "The engagement feast was held last month"—"Oh!"—towel drops to the floor |
| Suspended Ending | *The Circus Performer*: "Leave? Who said so? No, never! I cannot leave," alone on stage as brass music drifts in; *Should He Be Forgiven?*: artificial limb, asking the audience directly, "Should I forgive him?" |
| Extended Ending | *Oppression*: "Ah, what is your family name?" "I... ah... I..."; *The Seventh Magnolia*: benefactor remains unseen, flower offerings, lingering chorus |
| Rebound Ending | *The Tenor*: Helen shoots herself dead, followed by "I have one minute and ten seconds left... I must catch this train to sing in Brussels tomorrow night"; *Witness*: off-stage gunshot (in the style of O. Henry's *The Cop and the Anthem*) |
| Enlightening Ending | *The Man on the Cross*: Magdalene reflects, "The true fault was his own... perhaps he thought his dying would gain us something"; *Weighing the Mother-in-Law*: "Every heart holds its own scale" |
| Echo Ending | *Striking the Brass Gong*: Cai Jiu strikes three gongs with the same four lines—"Harvest season, grain like gold; every household, mind your chickens and ducks"—light first, heavy last |
| Cyclic Ending | *The Morrison Case*: "You made me an active citizen"—"That is where you err, I was not one before"—"Beware the wrath of the masses one day"; *Leading Lady Takes the Stage*: Annie locks away the index cards and resumes writing apologies: "Why did you extinguish the starlight?" |
| Conclusion Ending | *Three Dollars of National Currency*: "A drawn game"; *Ge Ma*: "Pay respects to the Zhang family of Dahong"; closing choruses in *Lullaby*, *The Irremovable Daughter-in-law*, *Pushing the Millstone Together* |

### Three Vehicles for Transformation
- Force of Environment: *The Law of Hospitality*: three greedy, snobbish customs clerks cannot hold back Peter; striking London laborers rally in support, compelling upper management to grant sweeping clearance.
- Force of Setup: *The Rising of the Moon*: ballad in three movements—love ballad → protest ballad → underground recognition signal; the sergeant's soliloquy: "Had I not joined the force back then, I might be the one fleeing prison and crouching in dark alleys right now..."
- Force of Action: *Ah Er Welcomes His Wife*: singing while weaving hampers: "A hamper stands on bamboo ribs just as A-Xiu anchors our home... together husband and wife weave a treasure bowl"; *The Irremovable Daughter-in-law*: tearing papers across three confessions (denouncing her husband to save kin / aborting to keep the home afloat / pawning her dowry to pay debts for medicine)—validating action because the exposition was dramatized earlier.

### Climax Examples
- Character-Driven: *Women's Representative*: Zhang Guirong moves from submissive endurance to snapping the whip and seizing land deeds: "Two deeds, one is mine; three rooms, one belongs to me... I am a human being, a citizen of this nation, not livestock fed by your hand! Do not forget the days of treating women like animals are gone forever," wrapping her child and striding out.
- Environment-Driven: *Zhao Xiaolan*: village chairman's recommendation letter → the Wei family son breaks off the betrothal → elder sister cries "Father, I am divorced, why shouldn't I celebrate?" → the mother straightens her back; *The Cop and the Thief*: the mock policeman steps into the role under the patrol officer's guidance, assisting a blind woman, offering directions, shouting "got one!" only to realize the other culprit is himself, handing over the stolen purse.
- Accidental Event: *The Stone Shrine*: a mother hoards coins to erect a shrine for her "gloriously fallen" son; the son unexpectedly returns demanding cash; the mother summons the patrol: "This man is a thief and a murderer without identification papers"; dragged away, the son cries "Woman, have mercy on your own son!"; the mother collapses dead before the icon: "What is a single life weighed against an ideal?"
- Obligatory Scene Example: *The Splendor of Socks*: burning fifty thousand pairs of substandard socks → Robert seals a twelve-million-dollar purchase order → uncle admits "I only burned several crates, packing the rest with old newspapers" → "Two options: burn them or vacate the post" → "Three terms: I retain executive power, development authority, and personnel control" → "I am no uncle of yours!" → modeling troupe call-and-response song: "Was my heart too ruthless?" "Not ruthless, but filled with deep love for Shasha."

## VII. Hicks's Three-Act Outline for *Rain Man* (Attraction / Anticipation / Satisfaction)

- Act I: Charlie discovers his late father's three-million-dollar estate went into an unknown trust; investigating the institution, he discovers an autistic older brother, Raymond—Attraction: A self-absorbed man learns he has been disinherited and saddled with an unknown brother.
- Act II: Charlie "kidnaps" Raymond across the country to leverage half the inheritance; the audience anticipates he will exploit Raymond (card-counting in Las Vegas) while also anticipating he will change; every stop along the highway subverts expectations while seeding new ones.
- Act III: At the custody hearing, Charlie gives up custody of Raymond—Satisfaction: He walks away not with money, but with a brother; expectations are fulfilled, though not in the manner the audience imagined.
- Hicks's "Minimal Action" Exercise: Stanley and the dog—revealing who a character is through the fewest possible physical movements; the "Eight-Question Wonder Test": pose eight specific questions to each character; only when the answers surprise the author does the character come alive.

## VIII. Index of Structural Failures

- Overlong Setup: *Minority Report* takes 40 minutes to set its hook; *Along Came Polly* exhaustively dramatizes the wedding, the honeymoon, and the infidelity in full.
- Sagging Act II Belly: Repeating Act I stakes and scale of action (McKee); "The Cat in the Hat running in circles without anything actually changing" (Snyder's "turn, turn, turn").
- Contrived Climax: Five short-form plays in the Shanghai showcase all resolving through kneeling (Lu Jun).
- Weak Transformation: Depending on a phone call from the municipal party secretary (*After the Bell Rings*), an imported Soviet film (*Household Affairs*), or a staged poisoning (stale contrivance).
- Overly Neat Resolution: Laid-off model workers finding bliss as short-order cooks; dragged-out endings: environmental exhibits resembling green mushroom lectures; jarring tone shifts: bird fights backed by the *Yellow River Cantata*.
- Coincidental Climax and Resolution: Sudden hurricanes, stampeding elephants, traffic wrecks, or a T-Rex showing up (McKee's *Deus ex Machina*).
- Underpowered Antagonist: When the protagonist is Superman, they must be reduced to an underdog (choosing the lesser of two evils between two nuclear warheads → Lois dies → reversing Earth's rotation).
- Double Magic: *Spider-Man* pairing radioactive spiders with biochemical accidents; *Signs* combining extraterrestrials with a crisis of faith (Snyder).
- The Glacier Problem: Danger approaching so slowly that stakes evaporate (volcano threatening the town only reaching the villagers by page 75).
