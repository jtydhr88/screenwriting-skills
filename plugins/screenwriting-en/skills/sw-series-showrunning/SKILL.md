# Series design (剧集设计)

Owners: `sw-story-structure` general Yorke structure; `sw-premise-theme` premise/theme; `sw-character-conflict` character; `sw-dialogue` dialogue; `sw-scene-craft` hooks and scene revision; `sw-format-adaptation` storyline documents, proposals, script format; `sw-industry-business` writers room, production, rewrites; `sw-workflow` stages and saved state.

This skill designs a series that can keep generating episodes. Pamela Douglas's four-act grid is a planning tool, not a law. Five-act and six-act patterns are separate options. Study the show you are writing.

## I. Engine, not a long movie

William Rabkin: a series is not a long movie chopped into chapters. Movie questions want solutions. Series questions want processes that can iterate. His Extant example is a hook that tests like a movie (astronaut returns pregnant) but a series pitch about fleeing government baby-thieves can lose the dial. Use that imagined dial: interest in the hook versus interest in the weekly show.

Neil Landau: franchise is the week-to-week verb. Name what the regulars do each episode, not only who they are. Practical test: at least six future episode ideas beyond the pilot. Trouble generating six can mean a limited premise. Premise pilots start day one of the premise (Lost crash, Grey's first internship day, X-Files partnership, Homeland Brody's return). Non-premise pilots drop into a world already running (Mad Men, Game of Thrones, Downton Abbey, The West Wing). Hybrids may open on a climax then flash forward or back. A premise pilot is a category, not a failed engine.

Landau: closed-ended weeks resolve the case, verdict, or cure in the hour. Serialized weeks are ongoing character plotlines (24, Dallas, Breaking Bad, Mad Men). Hybrids carry both (The Good Wife, Grey's Anatomy). Case-of-the-week lives in investigating, diagnosing, litigating, arresting. Serialized franchises live in cumulative character arcs. Name form separately from franchise: series, serial, soap; closed, serialized, or hybrid.

Yvonne Grace on form: a series (Casualty-type) can open and return around a standing cast and precinct. A serial aims at an overarching resolution across parts. Soaps (Coronation Street, EastEnders, Emmerdale) have no definable ending.

Landau arena is setting plus period, geography, customs, vernacular, values. Research until the world yields specific story engines. Mythology is the rulebook of the game. Central mystery looks backward (what happened and why). Central question looks forward (how will this be solved). Keep supernatural rules simple and consistent; Lost's expanding flashback/flash-forward/sideways book is a caution. Sweet spot is the weekly intersection of genre, tone, theme, conflict, franchise, and question/mystery. A satisfying pilot ending is an earned aha: discovery you should have seen coming and did not.

Rabkin franchise: concept, conflict, and theme must match or every episode starts from zero. The repeated pattern is the chord changes; weekly plots are the melody. Sons of Anarchy: Jax protecting three families whose interests oppose. Breaking Bad pattern: Walt solves a problem, the solution collides with people more evil, the next solution makes him more evil. Plant character-conflict landmines in subtext so later episodes detonate what the pilot implied. Do not clutter the pilot's core with every future conflict. Outlet caution: a cool reveal parked at the end of act four often belongs earlier if that pace is needed; binge viewing exposes mechanical resets that weekly airing hid. Cadence caution, not a universal speed rule.

John Yorke: successful series have a renewable story of the week, format rules, status order, and lovable regulars. Drama wants change; returning series often need characters to stay usable. Tools: small, two-dimensional, or temporary change. Selective amnesia is an observed tactic with a cost, not an instruction to break Chris Curry's canon. Needs and flaws overcome only fleetingly, or not until the last episode, or the engine dies (Moonlighting/Frasier coupling warning). Jumping the shark is sensation after the engine is empty. Finite serials may complete. Renewal series should leave process alive and explain the ongoing source.

## II. Pilot and season

Landau’s six springboards test the engine rather than prove it. Rabkin: keep the audience on the process, not a movie answer in episode one.

Douglas: do not spec episodes that change the central character or the course of the show. Guest-driven stories that would work as movies without the regulars fail the series test. Jane-and-Sally burglar contrast: the regulars must own jeopardy and turning points.

Optional Douglas season method: color-coded horizontal character lines across episodes, then vertical slices where arcs intersect. Other rooms collect facts episode by episode (her Northern Exposure note). Jason Katims, in Landau, maps vague character journeys, then breaks movements of several episodes around a question, then raises a new question. Yorke's Criminal Justice five-episode reading (episode one as series inciting incident, midpoint demand for truth, penultimate worst point, finale revelation) is fractal season shape, not a mandatory template. Distinguish season transformation from episode transformation.

## III. Episode grid, beats, A/B/C

Douglas: put away three-act feature books for this planning. Network hours long used four acts toward commercial breaks as hard curtains. Many broadcast shows later used five acts; a few used six. Premium cable may have no aired breaks and can plan more like a movie. Writers may still plan four acts even when later drafts add breaks. Four-act shows may still use teasers; tags are optional actual format. Study the show.

Douglas four-act planning pages (first draft, with teaser): Act One around 17–18, Act Two around 30, Act Three around 45, Act Four around 50–60. Seven numbered slots per act are optional two-minute scene arithmetic, not a quota. Five solid scenes can fill an act; vignette shows may run nine or ten. Her ~28 scenes is illustration. Beats are dramatic steps, not slugline counts. A slug line is a production heading; an establishing shot is not a dramatic scene. A beat may span several slugs. A new conflict in the same location is a new outline beat.

Douglas: A is the largest story, B second, C often runner or relief. Weights flex: equal A and B, C seeding a later arc, two or four stories, The Wire many, Dexter primarily A. Conditional illustration only: A 12–16, B 8–12, C 3–4 up to 8. Not quotas.

Tent-poles Douglas uses, in this order: opening, ending, Act Three worst case (whatever opposes the protagonist's goal), then Act One and Act Two cliffhangers. Reverse from the break. Credibility and rooting interest are her two tests. Checkerboard weave has no required pattern; an act-end cliff may be B; C may start in Act Two; a line may end in Act Three.

Five-act and six-act notes and teaser examples live in [reference.md](reference.md). Hour scripts Douglas cites often 50–60 pages; some talk-heavy hours ran longer; five-act-plus-teaser hours can land near 48 with ~8-page acts. Time the draft; trim or add a real beat. Production deadline culture belongs to `sw-industry-business`.

## IV. Serial storylines, soap continuity, bible

Yvonne Grace: know each storyline's natural length. Rank and connect them. A moment is not a storyline; expand the springboard until someone drives a journey. Rank: A is central to this episode; B has most impact or resonance; C undercuts, contrasts, or augments. Soaps often run four or five concurrent lines over a week. Design order: through-line, then broad episodes the line occupies, then affected characters, then each journey, then connections and parallels. Long-run craft is crossovers and counterpoints. Detailed storyline document layout lives in `sw-format-adaptation`. Hand off design here.

Chris Curry: adjacent episodes sit on different emotional steps. The starting point must pick up the previous episode’s hook; the destination is the predetermined hook of the episode in hand. At a returning character’s first appearance, remind the ongoing mood with a note or stage direction. Established set facts and canon bind; soap history is sacrosanct. Soap often telescopes time to keep momentum, but chronology is retained.

Yorke selective amnesia is observation of how some returning shows reset; Curry canon still records what happened.

Internal series bible is a workflow record, not a published book template. Named primary fields: Engine, Arena, Franchise, Season arc, Episode grid, plus Form and Pilot. Save via `sw-workflow`.

## Workflow

Stages remain 0 launch/resume, 1 premise, 2 structure, 3 character, 4 scenes/treatment, 5 draft including dialogue, 6 revision, 7 submission/industry. Series craft sits inside those stages; it is not extra stages. Saved bible and grid paths: `sw-workflow`.

1. Engine test: process question, franchise verbs, arena, six springboards, Rabkin dial, Landau sweet spot. Premise/theme with `sw-premise-theme`.
2. Pilot type and season movements. Episode uses the show's real act pattern. General shape with `sw-story-structure`.
3. Regulars and conflict engine with `sw-character-conflict`. Plant landmines.
4. Break A/B/C on the grid; Grace natural lengths; Curry pickup/handoff. Document format in `sw-format-adaptation`. Scenes in `sw-scene-craft`.
5. Draft including dialogue (`sw-dialogue`). Keep beat vs slug distinction.
6. Revise against the diagnostic checklist.
7. Submission and industry path: `sw-industry-business`.

## Diagnostic checklist

- Movie solution or weekly process (Rabkin)?
- Six springboards without exploding the premise (Landau)?
- Franchise verbs named (Landau)?
- Arena specific enough to generate story (Landau)?
- Mystery (past) vs question (future) vs mythology rules (Landau) consistent?
- Pilot premise or non-premise, ending an earned discovery (Landau)?
- Season movement vs this episode's grid (Douglas/Katims/Yorke), not one template?
- Actual act count of the show (Douglas)?
- Beats vs slugs vs dramatic scenes (Douglas)?
- A/B/C weights illustrative, not quotas (Douglas)?
- Regulars drive turns, not guests (Douglas)?
- Natural storyline lengths ranked and connected (Grace)?
- Cliff accounted for and different emotional step on first appearance of a continuing line (Curry)?
- Canon vs Yorke-observed amnesia cost?
- Outlet cadence considered, not a speed dogma (Rabkin)?
