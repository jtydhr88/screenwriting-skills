> The reference material for Chinese series practice is split across four files: [reference.md](reference.md) (§1–8: the taxonomy, planning documents, script format, per-episode metrics, adaptation, fragment adaptation, the production chain and censorship, the terminology dictionary) / [reference-samples.md](reference-samples.md) (§9–12: samples of the four formats, sample planning documents, a worked per-episode scene table, the 36 situations and the 36 comic situations) / [reference-cases.md](reference-cases.md) (§13: Zhang Wei's six adaptation cases) / this file (§14–15: Zhao Binbin's index of 26 fragments, and the *General Rules* red-line table).

## 14. The Index of Zhao Binbin's 26 Fragments

Source: chapter 4 of Zhao Binbin's *A Course in Adapting Film and Television Fragments* (6 worked examples with scripts plus teaching notes, p0024–0083) and chapter 5, "Practice Material" (20 fragments, p0084–0197). **26 fragments** in all. The direction is the exact opposite of §13: **a finished film → an 8–20 minute stage fragment for 3–8 people** (rehearsal material for vocational-college acting programmes). The method is set out in [reference.md](reference.md) §6.

### 14.3 Configuration Statistics (the two sets of statistics on Zhao p0141 and p0197 combined)

- **Cast of 3–7** (fewest 3: *Bodyguards and Assassins*; most 9: *The Women of the Knifeman's Family* and *The Fragrance of Herbs (2)*).
- **One main setting** (with an occasional adjoining second zone: outside the door / a corridor / a dark room); **the only three-zone set** is *Pretty Big Feet*, and **the only contrasting two-zone set** is *Eighteen Springs*.
- **Subject distribution (the 20 examples of chapter 5)**: Republican mansion / period drama 11; the war of resistance 3; contemporary family 4; countryside / period youth 2; palace intrigue 1. → **Period drama predominates overwhelmingly** (8 of the 10 examples in the fourth set of notes are period dramas).
- **Where the passage is taken from (the 20 examples of chapter 5)**: episode 1, 7; episode 2, 4; episodes 4–7, 5; after episode 20, 2; unmarked, 2. → "**Strong-conflict scenes that can be rehearsed independently**" are heavily concentrated in **the first five episodes of a series.**
- **The proportion adapted from novels at second hand**: at least 10 of the 20 have a novel as the ultimate source (Eileen Chang 2, Yan Geling 2, Qiong Yao 2, Fei Wo Si Cun, Qiu Shanshan, Liu Lianzi, Dong Luming), so **most of the book is a three-stage adaptation, novel → series → stage**; and every stage is compressing — the exact opposite of Zhang Wei's "novel → long-form = expansion."
- **The law of closing**: **it must be a visible bodily event or a departure** — jumping into the pond / kneeling / weeping / legs giving way / an elder's dressing-down dominating the stage / watching a farewell / escaping through a window / everyone dead plus kneeling downstage / carrying someone out on your back / bleeding and fainting / dashing your head against a wall / shutting and locking a door.

### 14.4 Representative Dialogue Examples (transcribed, as a corpus)

#### Example 1, *Love in a Fallen City*: asking for money by the circuitous route (Zhao p0111) — a model of the Chinese "talking around it"

```
THIRD SISTER-IN-LAW: (sitting down) It was Mother, after all, who thought it
      through, saw clearly, and handed me the household accounts. If I did not
      manage them, who knows what would happen. Ah — Sixth Sister, it has been
      several days now since you came back from your husband's family, hasn't it?
BAI LIUSU: (embarrassed) It has. I'm sorry to have troubled you.
THIRD SISTER-IN-LAW: No trouble at all. (having said it, she looks Liusu over)
      In a household this size, there are seven things to see to the moment you
      open the door — firewood, rice, oil, salt, sauce, vinegar and tea — and if I
      look after any one of them carelessly, there's a hole in the accounts at the
      month's end. Sixth Sister, you know this household is not what it was; one
      more person means one more mouth. Those on the books are easy enough; those
      not on the books — (with a small laugh) as your sister-in-law, to open my
      mouth and ask you for board money is truly embarrassing. But if I do not ask,
      there will be talk. (gesturing toward the door)
```

→ The technique: **a four-step circuit** — establish authority (Mother gave me the accounts) → ask how long (it's been several days now) → speak of the common difficulty (seven things at the door) → **hand responsibility for the demand to an absent "somebody else"** (gesturing toward the door). **She never has to strike the final blow herself.**

#### Example 2, *Mother*: the war over what to call someone (Zhao p0104–0108) — one word carrying the whole antagonism

```
MANZHANG'S MOTHER: Isn't that Jindou? So you're back from the Japanese?!
JINDOU: Manzhang's mother, use my proper name! (arrogantly)
MANZHANG'S MOTHER: Aiya, by the generations you ought to be calling me auntie.
JINDOU: Hmph. The world has changed now, there's no need for all that fuss.
MANZHANG'S MOTHER: However the world changes, the ancestors don't change.
  ……
MANZHANG'S MOTHER: Jindou—
JINDOU: Address me by my proper name! Kawayama Ichiro! (hands on hips, chin up,
      swaggering)
MRS MENG: Ichiro or Jiro — your name is Deyong! (turning in alarm, ashamed)
MANZHANG'S MOTHER: Deyong, Deyong, look — my whole family and this auntie of
      yours, aren't we all kneeling here before you—
```

→ The technique: a collaborator's change of name is fought over by **his mother, his neighbour and himself**, so that "Deyong / Jindou / Kawayama Ichiro" are three claims to identity; and when Manzhang's mother finally shouts "Deyong," it is both submission and humiliation. **More effective than any line about national righteousness.**

#### Example 3, *Tie Lihua*: interrogation in the passage (Zhao p0121) — writing "stalling" as a scene in its own right

```
FIRST WIFE: You walk so fast, how could I catch sight of you! It's late — what is
      it that's so urgent!
ZHANG JI'AN: (hesitating) Oh, I haven't seen Auntie for days. I remember she told
      me last time that she likes the walnut cakes from Old Sun's, (holding up the
      package) and I happened to pass by, so I bought her some.
FIRST WIFE: Then why are you going this way? You come and go in this house all the
      time — have you forgotten where the old lady lives?
ZHANG JI'AN: (with a laugh) Listen to you, Sister-in-law — how could I forget where
      the old lady lives! (glancing around and stepping closer to her) I suddenly
      needed the privy.
FIRST WIFE: The fourth and fifth wives live over that way. Whose courtyard were you
      planning to use the privy in!?
  ……
FIRST WIFE: By the way — let me carry the cakes for you.
[Zhang Ji'an freezes]
FIRST WIFE: I'm going to the old lady's anyway. You can hardly take pastries into
      the privy!
[Zhang Ji'an turns and exits. The first wife rummages through the package, finds
nothing suspicious, and leaves]
```

→ The technique: **every lie is dismantled on the spot with ordinary common sense** (the wrong route, the location of the privy, taking pastries to the lavatory), and the physical evidence is finally confiscated with "let me carry it for you." In plot terms this scene is nothing but "a reason for being late," **and on stage it is a complete attack and defence.**

#### Example 4, *Ma Wen's War*: seven inversions of the empty word "interesting" (Zhao p0064–0070) — a comic weapon of Chinese dialogue

```
YANG XIN: Ma Wen, tell me — is there anything interesting about us living like this?
MA WEN: Well! It's interesting!
  ……
MA WEN: How uninteresting that would be! I've thought about it too — this really
      isn't interesting, and it makes me look like a man with no sense of what's
      interesting! But then, living like this, it's got interesting!! Very
      interesting! Interesting beyond words!
YANG XIN: Interesting, is it!? I'll make it more interesting for you! Your ex-wife
      is certainly interesting enough! (shutting the door)
```

→ The technique: **seize on one empty word and invert it repeatedly** ("interesting" seven times), moving inside a single speech from "uninteresting" to "interesting enough." The same fragment also has **the self-deprecating counter-attack**: "Vulgar! Of course I'm vulgar! **If I weren't vulgar, why would you have divorced me?!**"; and **the threat of time**: "she used to be my wife, now she's yours, but **who knows whose wife she'll be in the future — and where will you be living then?**"

#### Example 5, *Coming Home*: the three "organization" questions (Zhao p0059) — the most economical "impossible to communicate" on stage

```
DIRECTOR LI: Comrade Feng Wanyu, do you believe in the organization?
FENG WANYU: I do.
DIRECTOR LI: Do I represent the organization?
[Feng Wanyu nods]
DIRECTOR LI: Then should you not believe me?
FENG WANYU: I should.
DIRECTOR LI: Good. (putting the official letter into her bag, standing and pulling
      Lu Yanshi forward) I now guarantee you in the name of the organization that
      the man standing in front of you is your husband, Comrade Lu Yanshi!
FENG WANYU: I know who he is. His name is Fang. He is Master Fang!
```

→ The technique: **three escalating closed questions plus one answer that negates the whole thing.** Each question admits only "yes," pressing the other party into a logical corner, and then she negates every premise from an entirely different level — **the antagonism is not inside the logic, it is inside the cognition.**

### 14.5 The Delivery Format (reverse-engineered from the uniform convention of the 26 fragments, Zhao p0024–0197; format notes at p0197)

The fixed order: **the title plus "adapted from the film / series X of the same name"** → **one paragraph describing the zoned set** → **time / place / characters** (one line of biography each, which may include "where the character goes later") → **a story summary** (3–6 lines, writing "what happened in the 2 minutes before the fragment starts") → **the script** (`【】` action cues on their own lines, `CHARACTER: (parenthetical) line`) → `reference video source: episode N of the series X` plus `note: adapted from <author>'s novel X` or `…written by <name>`.

**No scene numbers, no interior/exterior/day/night, and no camera terminology or transitions at all.** Seven supplementary conventions (p0197): ① `【off-stage voice】` has two uses — an offstage event (smashing the open flame in *The Women of the Knifeman's Family*) and a character's interior monologue (*Eighteen Springs*); ② `【scene change, memory】…【scene change】` for a stage flashback; ③ `【music in】/【music rising】/【music fading】/【music swelling】` — the book's only rhythm markings, occurring fixedly at the opening, before the emotional peak, and at the close, and **almost all 20 fragments end on "【music in】/【music rising】"**; ④ foreign lines = the original plus a bracketed literal Chinese translation; ⑤ breaking the fourth wall is permitted (`[running from the auditorium up onto the stage]`, `[the main curtain slowly opens]`, `(to a member of the audience)`) to solve a shortage of supporting actors; ⑥ crowd roles are numbered (`schoolgirl A/B/C/D`, `maid A/B`); ⑦ a multi-person melee gets one overall staging note.

⚠️ The two conflicts with `sw-format-adaptation` are flagged at the end of [reference.md](reference.md) §6 ("you must describe the story in shots" vs. "never mention the camera"; and a character list with biographies being mandatory in fragment and Chinese industry drafts and explicitly forbidden in Anglo-American specs). One more: item 5 of the *Aftershock* teaching note (p0054) encourages "speaking the lines in a Tangshan-accented dialect" — **but not one of the book's 26 scripts transliterates dialect anywhere**, so the two are in fact consistent: **dialect belongs to the performance layer and does not enter the script's text.**

## 15. The *General Rules for Television Drama Content Production* Red-Line Classification (**the 31 December 2015 version**)

Source: appendix 1 (p0291–0294) of Zhang Mingzhi and Song Peiyi's *A Course for Television Drama Producers and Executive Producers*, transcribed in full. **Issued by**: the Television Production Committee of the China Radio and Television Association and the China Television Drama Production Industry Association. **Basis**: the *Provisions on the Administration of Television Content* of the State Administration of Press, Publication, Radio, Film and Television. **Nature**: an industry self-regulation code (not an administrative regulation), "to guide production units in avoiding risk and producing soundly." **Version year: 31 December 2015.**

⚠️ **Every item here is the text of the 31 December 2015 version.** Since then the competent authorities have issued further documents (including regulation of "danmei adaptations," "idol cultivation" and "fandom"), and items have been added and removed; **the framework and phrasing of the ten classes remain in use, but any citation must carry a year and be checked against current documents.** The table below expands article 5's ten classes into self-checkable items — **the "self-check question" column is not the original text but a rewriting of the provision as a question to ask while reading a draft; only the "provision" column is the 31 December 2015 original.**

An overview (article 4, the 31 December 2015 version, p0291): **the eleven broad prohibitions on what may not be produced** = (1) violating the basic principles established by the constitution, or inciting resistance to or the destruction of the implementation of the constitution, laws, administrative regulations and rules; (2) endangering national unity, sovereignty and territorial integrity; (3) divulging state secrets, endangering national security, or damaging national honour and interests; (4) inciting ethnic hatred or discrimination, infringing ethnic customs, hurting ethnic feelings, or destroying ethnic unity; (5) contravening national religious policy, promoting religious extremism, cults and superstition, or discriminating against and insulting religious belief; (6) disrupting social order and destroying social stability; (7) promoting obscenity, gambling, violence, terror or drug use, inciting crime or teaching methods of committing it; (8) insulting or defaming others; (9) endangering social morality or the fine cultural traditions of the nation; (10) infringing minors' lawful rights and interests, or harming minors' physical and mental health; (11) other content prohibited by law, administrative regulation and rules.

### 15.1 Class One: Not in Keeping With National Conditions and the Social System, Damaging to the National Image, Endangering National Unity and Social Stability (the 31 December 2015 version, 8 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 1-1 | Belittling the national image, the national system, or principles and policies | Does any line or plot in this draft make a system or policy itself the object of ridicule? |
| 1-2 | Damaging the public image of **specific professions and groups such as the people's army, armed police, state security, public security and judicial personnel, and of social organizations and bodies** | Among the police, soldiers, judges and doctors I have written, is any of them a villain or a clown? Is their profession used as a negative joke? (Note: such content also triggers article 6's expert vetting; see 15.11) |
| 1-3 | **Dwelling on and exaggerating social problems, over-representing and displaying the dark side of society** | Is the show's worldview "there are problems and they are being addressed," or "rotten to the core"? Is there a whole line that exists only to prove society is beyond saving? |
| 1-4 | Belittling the people's role in driving historical development | Are the historical turns driven solely by one or two heroes or grandees, or do the people have a place? |
| 1-5 | ★**Taking a negative character as the main object of portrayal, or writing a biography or a eulogy for reactionary, backward, evil or illegal social forces, organizations and figures, emphasizing their positive side** | Is the POV character a villain? Is there an "anti-hero as protagonist" premise? — **this item directly vetoes the American anti-hero-lead drama** |
| 1-6 | Promoting the armed conquest of foreign peoples by China's feudal dynasties | In a costume drama, is a foreign war written as "the glory of conquest"? |
| 1-7 | Promoting lines, forms of address and images with a colonialist colouring | Is any colonial-era form of address or insignia used positively? |
| 1-8 | ★**Detached from national conditions, lacking a basic grounding in real life, promoting luxurious living, and the like** | Do the protagonist's housing, income and spending match their status? Is there a floating "has a car, has a flat, both parents dead" premise? (Compare Zhang Wei's identical criticism of idol drama at p0089, §13.1 of reference-cases.md) |

### 15.2 Class Two: Damaging to Ethnic Unity (the 31 December 2015 version, 4 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 2-1 | Plots, lines, forms of address, character images, pictures and sound effects that hurt ethnic feelings | Is there any pejorative term or stereotyped image of an ethnic group? |
| 2-2 | Sensationalizing distinctive ethnic customs and religious beliefs as exotica, or even uglifying and insulting them | Are ethnic customs written as part of the characters' life logic, or as spectacle? |
| 2-3 | Depicting inter-ethnic wars or historical events that hurt ethnic feelings | Is the subject itself a war between ethnic groups inside China? |
| 2-4 | ★**Presenting historical campaigns between ethnic groups as wars between states** | In a costume war drama, are the two sides called "states" or "tribes"? How are lines like "the enemy state" and "the fall of the state" handled? |

### 15.3 Class Three: Contrary to National Religious Policy (the 31 December 2015 version, 4 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 3-1 | Promoting religious extremism and cults | — |
| 3-2 | Inappropriately comparing the merits of different religions or denominations in a way that may provoke conflict between them | Does a character of one denomination prove another wrong? |
| 3-3 | Excessively displaying and promoting religious doctrine, rules and ritual | Do the ritual scenes run longer than the plot requires? |
| 3-4 | **Playfully distorting or mocking religious content** | Are monks, Taoists or clergy written as comic roles? |

### 15.4 Class Four: Promoting Feudal Superstition, Contrary to the Scientific Spirit (the 31 December 2015 version, 2 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 4-1 | ★**Promoting feudal superstitions such as spirit possession, reincarnation and witchcraft** | Does my premise contain time travel, rebirth, body-snatching, mediumship or spellcasting? If so, is it treated inside the show as a real mechanism, or explained away and denied? |
| 4-2 | Promoting the dross of feudal culture, such as ignorance, evil and the grotesque | Are customs such as marriage-to-cure-illness, life-swapping, live burial and immolation criticized, or treated as legitimate means? |

→ ⚠️ **This class collides head-on with the standard premises of costume, xianxia and mythological drama, and is the core risk point for time-travel and rebirth subjects.** The *Tie Lihua* case in Zhang Wei's book made one concrete workaround on exactly this basis: **removing Tie Lihua's "mediumship" and rewriting the tomb-robber identity as a deception** (Zhang Wei p0243–0245).

### 15.5 Class Five: Dwelling on Terror and Violence, Displaying Ugly Behaviour, Possibly Even Inducing Crime (the 31 December 2015 version, 8 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 5-1 | Dwelling on violence and homicide, presenting the rampancy of criminal and gang forces | How long does the passage in which the gang has the upper hand run? Is there an element of relish in it? |
| 5-2 | **Depicting a brutal and cruel criminal process in detail, and physical and psychological torture** | How many steps of the crime are written? Is the torture dispatched in a line or written out in full? |
| 5-3 | ★**Exposing investigative methods and detection detail, which may help criminals master counter-investigation techniques** | Does my police material write reproducible technical detail (evidence collection, surveillance deployment, interrogation technique, technical investigation)? |
| 5-4 | Depicting bizarre and grotesque criminal cases | Is the case designed to be sensational for its own sake? |
| 5-5 | ★**Blurring the value judgement between true and false, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, and confusing the basic boundary between the just and the unjust** | Does the show have a clear moral measure? Is a morally grey protagonist finally judged by the script? |
| 5-6 | Displaying drug use, drunkenness, gambling and other harmful behaviour in detail | Are the mechanics of drug use or gambling visible? (See also class nine's specific provision on smoking shots) |
| 5-7 | Excessive fright and terror, physical pain or hysteria — images, lines, music and sound effects that produce strong sensory and psychological stimulation and may cause physical or mental discomfort | Is there any passage that wins by physiological discomfort? |
| 5-8 | **Promoting meeting violence with violence, and dwelling on extreme vengeful psychology and behaviour** | Is the protagonist's revenge permitted, or constrained by a cost? |

→ ⚠️ **5-3 and 5-5 directly constrain how police and crime dramas are written: you cannot write the technical detail of solving a case, and you cannot write a morally grey anti-hero.** Read together with 5-5, situation 6 of the 36 situations in §12, "**revenge**" (Yao p0353–0354), must come with an adjudicating mechanism in a Chinese series — see the "**reward-and-punishment mechanism**" in §13.5 (Zhang Wei p0232–0233).

### 15.6 Class Six: Dwelling on Obscenity, Pornography and Vulgar Low Taste (the 31 December 2015 version, 8 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 6-1 | Concretely depicting prostitution, soliciting, promiscuity, rape and similar ugly behaviour | Do these acts occur on stage? Could they be moved offstage with only the consequences visible? |
| 6-2 | ★**Depicting and displaying irregular sexual relationships and acts, such as incest, homosexuality, sexual perversion, sexual assault, sexual abuse and sexual violence** | (**This is the source of "homosexuality" being explicitly listed as prohibited; this is the 31 December 2015 original wording**) |
| 6-3 | ★**Displaying and promoting unhealthy views and states of marriage and romance, such as extramarital affairs, one-night stands and sexual freedom** | Is the affair the premise of the plot, or the object of criticism? Could it be changed to "both parties already divorced"? |
| 6-4 | Numerous shots giving sensory stimulation, and comparable indirect depictions or implications of sexual acts | — |
| 6-5 | Images, lines, music and sound effects with obvious sexual provocation, harassment or humiliation, or comparable effects | — |
| 6-6 | Displaying male and female genitalia and other private areas, and excessively revealing clothing | — |
| 6-7 | Using coarse language and the like | Is there swearing or vulgar punning? (In Zhao Binbin's *My Best Gay Friend* fragment there is one vulgar pun; mark it as the original when using it as corpus — see §14, #15) |
| 6-8 | Sexual images, lines, music and sound effects unsuitable for minors | — |

→ **Zhang Wei's recorded workarounds for 6-3 and 6-2** (already listed in [reference.md](reference.md) §7.5; only the correspondence is marked here): *Ma Wen's War* deleted every trace of adultery and reset Li Yi as an already divorced single man (Zhang Wei p0122); *Cell Phone* turned Wu Yue into an editor whose feelings "stop at propriety," and turned Fei Mo's improper romance with a female graduate student into "the female graduate student falls in love with his son" (Zhang Wei p0066, with the admitted cost: "**changes like these do weaken the film's sharpness and impact**"); and *Aftershock*, adapted from the novella, cut "the adoptive father's rape" and "replaced it with an adoptive father's deep love for his adopted daughter" (Zhang Wei p0028).

### 15.7 Class Seven: Insulting or Defaming Others (the 31 December 2015 version, 2 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 7-1 | ★**Damaging the image and reputation of important historical figures or other real people, causing an adverse social effect** | Is there negative invention about any real person (or recognizable model) in the show? Has written consent been obtained from them or their close relatives? |
| 7-2 | Belittling others' professional identity, social status or physical characteristics | Is there a line using a physical characteristic or an occupation as a joke? |

→ The accompanying contractual obligation: when an adaptation involves documentary literature, real people or historical figures, the producer must warrant in writing that consent has been obtained from **the persons concerned or their close relatives**, with personality-rights disputes borne by the producer (screenwriter's contract 5.2.7, Zhang Mingzhi p0268; a 2016 model). The family risk in adapting a celebrity is in §13.3 (Zhang Wei p0157).

### 15.8 Class Eight: Distorting and Belittling the Fine Cultural Traditions of the Nation (the 31 December 2015 version, 5 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 8-1 | Dwelling on, exaggerating or concentrating on the nation's ignorance or social backwardness | Is backwardness the characters' circumstance, or the show's whole landscape? |
| 8-2 | ★**Contravening basic historical fact, "reversing the verdict" on historical figures and events already judged, or "vindicating" still-disputed historical figures and events** | Am I reversing the verdict on an already judged figure? Am I touching a still-disputed event? |
| 8-3 | ★**Tampering with a classic, distorting the source's spiritual substance** | When adapting a classic, what is the source's spiritual substance? Am I changing the structure or the core? (Compare "**adaptation is a higher form of original creation**" and "the three things that cannot be lost," Zhang Wei p0192–0193, in §13) |
| 8-4 | **Contravening basic historical common sense, lacking a basic historical basis, and arbitrarily distorting history** | Have the era, offices, objects and forms of address been checked? (This item also triggers article 6's expert vetting; see 15.11) |
| 8-5 | ★**Treating history, and especially revolutionary history, with excessive entertainment or playfulness** | Are there any "absurd drama" bits in the resistance or revolutionary material? |

→ **8-5 is the normative basis for the clean-up of "absurd anti-Japanese dramas."** The accompanying industry record: Zhang Mingzhi p0069 records that "**in an era that advocates harmony, presenting sharp conflict on screen is genuinely hard, so 'fighting the Japanese' became the dramatic conflict audiences enjoy and creators find easy to present**," noting that in **2013** the authorities had already moved to clean up "absurd anti-Japanese dramas." Another corresponding record: the reason for cutting *Drawing Sword*'s Cultural Revolution section, "**it involves the Cultural Revolution and cross-strait relations**" (Zhang Wei p0291–0292), is a compound risk of article 4's broad prohibitions (2) and (6) plus 8-2 here.

### 15.9 Class Nine: Endangering Social Morality and Having a Bad Influence on Minors (the 31 December 2015 version, 4 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 9-1 | Depicting minors' harmful behaviour such as early romance, smoking, drinking and fighting | How is the early-romance line in school material handled? (Compare §14, #6, the *Under the Hawthorn Tree* fragment: the mother's line "**I am not against free love, but I will not permit Jingqiu to have a romance this young**" is exactly this provision put into a character's mouth) |
| 9-2 | **Smoking shots and smoking scenes** contravening the relevant provisions of the State Council's broadcasting and film authority | How many times does the script write someone lighting a cigarette? (Note: this is a **specific provision**, not merely the general clause of class five's 5-6) |
| 9-3 | **Excessively exaggerated or bizarre character design** having a bad influence on minors | Is any costume or make-up description excessively exaggerated? |
| 9-4 | Other uncivil behaviour contrary to social morality | — |

### 15.10 Class Ten: Other Content Prohibited by Law, Regulation and State Provision (the 31 December 2015 version, 6 items)

| # | The provision (31 December 2015 original) | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| 10-1 | ★**Publicly displaying the internal systems and procedures of a specialized field, in contravention of relevant state provisions** | Are the internal procedures of the agency, army unit or court I have written non-public internal systems? (This and 5-3 on investigative methods are two separate provisions, and both must be checked) |
| 10-2 | Content that may provoke an international dispute or create an adverse international impression | Could the setting and lines involving foreign countries and regions be read as referring to real ones? |
| 10-3 | Misusing or misapplying specific insignia, call signs, forms of address and terminology, in contravention of relevant state provisions | Are the ranks, police numbers, channel logos, call signs and agency names accurate? (This also triggers article 6's expert vetting) |

(The accompanying contractual obligation: "Party A shall coordinate with the drama's **writer**, director, leads and other unit members to ensure that the placement appears in the drama as agreed between Party B and the advertising client"; and the creative script for the placement "**shall be submitted ＿ days before the drama starts shooting**" — Zhang Mingzhi p0307–0311, a 2016 model) |

### 15.11 Accompanying Provisions: Articles 6, 7 and 8 (the 31 December 2015 version)

- **Article 6, the three categories that must be vetted by experts (the 31 December 2015 version, p0294, as written)**: "treat the details of production with rigour and seriousness; where the following are involved, **relevant experts must be engaged to vet them**: (1) **minority languages and scripts, images of revolutionary pioneers and other important figures, the use of specific symbols and insignia, and graphics and charts**; (2) plots portraying **specific professions and groups such as the people's army, armed police, state security, public security, judicial personnel, doctors and lawyers, and social organizations and bodies**; (3) **the costumes, sets and props of specific historical periods and occupational groups.**"
  → ★ **A writer who touches any of these sends the script into "expert consultant reading,"** and time for revision must be left in the schedule; that money appears in the budget as "script discussion and consultancy fees" (Zhang Mingzhi p0145–0146). Content touching the law must also pass the unit's legal adviser (Zhang Mingzhi p0280).
- **Article 7, textual norms (the 31 December 2015 version, p0294, as written)**: "production units shall check the textual quality of a drama against **the national standards for language and script**, to ensure that characters and wording are correct and standard. Subtitles **shall be standard Chinese characters, other than a calligraphic title and related text.** Songs with lyrics, foreign-language titles and lines, and words and insignia with a specific meaning **shall carry Chinese subtitles.**"
- **Article 8, penalties (the 31 December 2015 version, p0294)**: where a violation of the rules causes an adverse effect, the association will, according to the circumstances, "impose criticism, warning, or notification to the whole industry and the public; and in serious cases **revoke membership and report to the competent authority for investigation and handling according to law.**"

### 15.12 Article 3's Eight Advocacies (the 31 December 2015 version, p0291) — the Positive Sell-Point List Outside the Prohibitions

Hold to the "two serves" direction and the "hundred flowers" policy, and produce outstanding works with "**an organic unity of thought, artistry and watchability**": (1) "hold to a people-centred creative orientation, take the people as the subject of representation, and reflect their voice"; (2) "hold to the leadership of core socialist values… and guide the people to establish and hold **a correct view of history, ethnicity, the state and culture**"; (3) ★**"take contemporary subjects as the main body, close to reality, close to life, close to the people, recording the development of the era and the progress of society."**; (4) "vigorously promote China's fine traditional culture. Take history as a mirror and pass on civilization"; (5) "exalt the true, the good and the beautiful, spread positive energy… and guide people to strengthen moral judgement and a sense of moral honour"; (6) "strive to tell China's story well, promote the Chinese spirit, and gather China's strength"; (7) "hold to putting social benefit first… and unify social and economic benefit"; (8) "firmly establish a consciousness of quality… and strive to produce outstanding works **profound in thought, superb in artistry and fine in production.**"

→ Of these, **item (3), "take contemporary subjects as the main body," most directly affects subject choice**, and corroborates the 2012 subject proportions (major subjects 1% / historical 43% / contemporary 56%, Zhang Mingzhi p0188, **the 2014–2016 picture**).

### 15.13 How to Use It: as a Reading Checklist

1. **Before project approval**, run 15.1 (1-5, 1-8), 15.4, 15.6 (6-2, 6-3) and 15.8 (8-2) — those five decide **whether the subject can be done at all**, and anything that cannot be changed means changing subjects.
2. **At the outline stage**, run 15.5 (5-5) and 15.8 (8-3) — those two decide **the moral measure and the source's core**, corresponding to "the three elements of the environment of thematic expression" in §13.5 (Zhang Wei p0232–0233).
3. **At the episode stage**, run 15.5 (5-1/5-2/5-3/5-8), 15.6 (6-1/6-4/6-5) and 15.9 — these are **scene-level** problems, mostly fixable with the workaround techniques in [reference.md](reference.md) §7.5, or convertible with Zhao Binbin's four steps of "offstage plus visible consequence" (see §14, #2).
4. **Before the lock**, run article 6's three categories in 15.11 — list every item in this draft that triggers expert vetting, and hand it to the producers together with the list of insignia, call signs and forms of address from 15.10 (10-3).
5. **Cite the year every time**: every provision in this table is the **31 December 2015 version**; current documents must be checked separately.
