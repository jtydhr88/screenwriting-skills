# Six Ozu Screenplays: Scene-by-Scene Breakdown and Original Text Examples

> Use in conjunction with SKILL.md. Source: *The Screenplays of Ozu Yasujiro (Chinese edition)*, translated by Wu Fei; scene numbers follow the original scripts.

## I. Late Spring (1949, 103 Scenes)

**Logline**: In Kamakura, widowed professor Shukichi (56), determined to nudge his reluctant daughter Noriko (27) out of the nest, lies that he plans to remarry; after anger and grief, Noriko consents to an arranged marriage, leaving her father peeling an apple alone on her wedding night.

| Sequence | Scene No. | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–8 | Kita-Kamakura Station, tea ceremony, garden shadows, bush warbler | Pillow shots establish season and stillness; Akiko enters frame (no dialogue) |
| Setup | 9–18 | Shukichi and Hattori drafting; Noriko returns; father and daughter ride train | "Pour tea, pour tea" cements domestic ease and mutual dependence |
| False Lead A | 21–26 | Ginza run-in with Onodera → Takigawa → calls remarriage "filthy" | Plant: Noriko explicitly rejects remarriage |
| False Lead B | 27–30 | Cycling with Hattori, stringy pickled radish | Misdirects audience to view Hattori as suitor |
| First Push | 31–37 | Aunt prods father to sound out Noriko on Hattori → "Hattori is getting married" | False lead collapses; comedic timing |
| Negative Space | 38–42 | Returning tickets at cafe → empty theater seat → Noriko walks Marunouchi alone | Ellipsis: empty seat articulates loneliness |
| Foil Enters | 43–48 | Aya visits: returned goods; school reunion gossip | "I won't marry" voiced aloud for the first time |
| Second Push | 49–52 | Aunt's house: arranged match proposed → rejected → suggests Akiko for father | Midpoint: Noriko coldly remarks, "I suppose so, if Father has no objections" |
| Cold War | 53–57 | Noriko ignores father; leaves forlornly: "Shopping..." | Virtually dialogue-free |
| Corroboration | 58–61 | Neighbor receives wedding gift from Hattori | External confirmation from third-party perspective |
| Pre-Climax | 62–66 | Noh theater: father nods to Akiko; Noriko darkens | Climax without dialogue |
| Outburst | 67–68 | Aya's place: quarrel over shortcake | Displacement |
| Showdown | 69–73 | "If someone took care of Father" → pressed → "Yes" → Noriko flees upstairs weeping | The lie settles |
| Release | 74 | Aunt finds wallet at Hachiman Shrine: "This marriage is meant to be" | Comic relief plus auspicious omen |
| Decision | 75–82 | Aya urges marriage → Noriko consents → father asks, "You haven't just given up?" | Reluctant concession |
| Farewell Journey | 83–93 | Kyoto: apologizing to Onodera; Ryoan-ji: "Raising a daughter is thankless"; inn night talk → monologue | Sole extended monologue in the film |
| Epilogue | 94–103 | Wedding day before mirror → kneeling farewell → empty room → Aya's kiss and "splendid lie" → severed apple peel → night sea | Truth laid bare by third party |

### Example A: Three "Yes"es (Scene 71)
```
Noriko: Then, Father, will you be like Uncle Onodera...
Shukichi: (Vaguely) Yes...
Noriko: Remarrying?
Shukichi: Yes...
Noriko: (Sharpening her tone) You will remarry, won't you?
Shukichi: Yes.
Noriko: That person from today?
Shukichi: Yes.
Noriko: Truly?... Is it true?
Shukichi: Yes.
Noriko: ... (Gradually unable to bear it)
She abruptly stands up and flees the room.
```

### Example B: Climax Without Dialogue (Scene 62, Noh Theater)
```
Shukichi looks at the libretto, then suddenly nods toward someone sitting opposite.
Noriko notices and looks over—
It is Miwa Akiko, seated across the hall.
Noriko also nods in greeting.
Akiko gracefully bows in return, without a word.
Shukichi returns his attention to the libretto and stage. Noriko, preoccupied by the silent exchange between her father and Akiko, cannot keep her eyes from drifting back toward Akiko.
Composed and elegant, Akiko watches the stage.
Though her father never looks toward Akiko again, and Akiko does not look back at her father, Noriko feels a deep unease rising within her.
Her expression grows steadily darker.
```

### Example C: Displaced Quarrel (Scene 68)
```
In a fit of pique, Noriko slams the plate holding the untouched cake onto the table with a clatter.
Aya: You're not eating?
Noriko: I don't want to!
Aya: Please eat it!
Noriko: I told you, I don't want to!
Aya: I'm telling you, it's delicious!
Noriko: Enough!
Aya: What is this over a little cake? I baked it myself, now eat it!
Noriko: I will not!
Aya: I told you to eat! You're eating it whether you want to or not!
Noriko: I just won't!
Aya: What's gotten into you? Fine, don't eat it!
Noriko: (So angry she cannot speak) ...
Aya: See? That's why I told you that you ought to hurry up and get married.
```

### Example D: The Sole Extended Monologue (Scene 93, Excerpt)
```
Shukichi: Father is already fifty-six, and my life is winding down, but yours is only beginning. ... That is simply the way human life goes.
... Even after you marry, you may not be happy right from the start. ... Happiness does not sit waiting for you; you must build it with your own hands. Marriage itself is not happiness—happiness lies in the process of two newlyweds building a new life together. ... Your mother was not happy right away either. ... Time and again, Father saw her crying in the corner of the kitchen. But your mother bore it and saw it through—
```

### Example E: Ending (Scenes 100–103)
```
Aya: I could never tell such a splendid lie.
Shukichi: What was that?
Aya: I could never tell such a splendid lie.
Shukichi: Hahahaha— (Then forlornly) What else could I do? It's the first time in my life I've ever told such a massive lie...
...
Alone, Shukichi somberly takes off his formal coat and hangs it on the doorframe peg. After patting off the dust, he walks limply over to a chair and sits down. Noticing an apple on the table, he picks it up to peel it, but the peel breaks before getting very far. Shukichi remains seated there, motionless.

103 The Night Sea
Broad, slow waves surge rhythmically against the shore and recede...
```

## II. Early Summer (1951, 145 Scenes)

**Logline**: In the three-generation Mamiya household in Kita-Kamakura, 28-year-old Noriko is offered an exceptionally favorable match through her company director; while the family investigates and lobbies her, she unexpectedly decides to marry Kenkichi—a widowed doctor with a child who is transferring to Akita—after his mother offhandedly remarks, "How wonderful it would be if you were my daughter-in-law." Baffled, the family accepts; they take a final portrait and scatter.

|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–19 | Morning routine, family breakfast, departing; grandfather trims grandson's nails | Single breakfast introduces three generations of seven |
| World | 20–25 | Director's office; Aya visits; siblings and sister-in-law eat tempura | Koichi's "urges you to drink but won't let you" summed up by his sister to his face |
| Uncle Visits | 26–45 | Hard-of-hearing Mokichi; boys yell curses; Great Buddha; Kabuki | Hard-of-hearing = family members unable to hear one another |
| Matchmaking | 40–46 | Takako and husband argue over carrots; director proposes match, shows golf photo | Married strife paired with unmarried matchmaking |
| Inquiry | 47–58 | Aya's mother brings news; Koichi and wife confer by bedside; Shukichi urges speed | Proposal advances in Noriko's absence |
| After Banquet | 59–61 | Debate over happy breed; Koichi peeks past shoji three times | Older brother directs wife from behind paper doors |
| Background Check | 62–64 | Credit agency visited; Shoji: "He won't be coming back" | Shifts from comedy into wartime trauma |
| Sunday | 65–75 | Carp streamers; boys lay tracks; elderly couple watches runaway balloon | Midpoint pillow shot: "Perhaps now is our family's finest hour" |
| Subplot Peak | 76–105 | 900-yen shortcake; tracks turn out to be bread → bread kicked → spanking → runaway → found | Adults offer gifts the children do not want |

### Example A: The Moment of Decision (Scene 113)
```
Tami: It's nothing, haha, just my own silly daydream. I was only thinking how wonderful it would be if a girl like you could be Kenkichi's bride!
Noriko: Oh.
Tami: Forgive me, it's just my foolish daydreaming... Don't be cross with me.
Noriko: Truly, Auntie?
Tami: What is it?
Noriko: Is that really what you think of me?
Tami: Forgive me, that's why I asked you not to be cross.
Noriko: Auntie, would an unmarried girl like me really do?
Tami: What? (As if doubting her own ears)
Noriko: If someone like me would do...
Tami: (Blurting out) Truly? (Her voice rising)
Noriko: Yes.
```

### Example B: Confirmation After the Fact (Scene 117)
```
Tami: Listen to me, Noriko said she's willing! She said she'd come to our home!
Kenkichi sits down heavily; Tami sits down too—
Kenkichi: Come where—
Tami: Come to our home!
Kenkichi: Come for what—
Tami: Not for what! Come to you! To be your wife!
Kenkichi: My wife?
Tami: Yes! Isn't it wonderful? It's marvelous...
Kenkichi: ... (Stunned)
...
Kenkichi: (Softly) I'm happy.
Tami: Well, you could show a little more joy on your face. Look pleased, now. What a strange boy you are...
```

### Example D: Theme in the Pillow Shot (Scene 75)
```
Shukichi: How quickly time passes... Koichi married, the grandchildren born, and now Noriko leaving. Perhaps right now is the happiest time of all.
Shige: Perhaps... though even after this...
Shukichi: Let's not dwell on it. Human greed knows no bounds. Ah, what a clear Sunday...
Shige: Oh, look— (Pointing to the sky across from them)
Shukichi: Hmm? (Looking up)
A balloon whose string has snapped drifts away into the sky.
Shukichi: Somewhere, there must be a child crying over a lost balloon... Didn't Koichi have days like that too...
```

### Example E: Catchphrase Relay in Ensemble Dialogue (Scene 40)
```
Aya: Husbands are all like that. That's why we don't want to get married. Right?
Noriko: Right. Wouldn't you say so?
Takako: Nonsense! You haven't even tried it!
Aya: Tried it?
Takako: You can't understand until you've been married!
Aya: By the time you understand, it's too late! Right?
Noriko: Right?
Takako: I might as well go home!
Aya: Absolutely, you should go home. Right?
Noriko: Exactly, goes without saying. Right?
Aya: After all, you did eat the carrots, right?
Takako: I'm not leaving!
```

```
Shukichi: (Suddenly gazing out the window, to Shige) Look, someone is marrying off their daughter.

Shige: I wonder where she's going...
Shukichi: Mmm...
Shige: How is Noriko doing right now, I wonder...
Shukichi: Mmm... Everyone has scattered, but we haven't done badly...

## III. Tokyo Story (1953, 176 Scenes)

**Logline**: Elderly couple Shukichi (70) and Tomi (67) travel from Onomichi to Tokyo to visit their children, only to be shuffled around by their busy offspring and shipped off to Atami; only Noriko, the widow of their second son killed in the war, treats them with genuine warmth. On the return journey, Tomi falls ill and dies shortly after reaching home; the children rush down for the funeral and scatter just as hastily, leaving only Noriko behind. The father gives her his late wife's watch, urging her to remarry.

| Sequence | Scene No. | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–6 | Onomichi morning market; packing air cushion; neighbor: "You're so fortunate" | Fortunate when seen from the outside |
| Arrival | 7–35 | Koichi's house: desk moved into hallway; Shige brings rice crackers; "Thought it would be livelier" | Son's home falls short of expectations |
| Disappointing Sunday | 36–52 | Koichi called away; Minoru throws tantrum; "By the time you're a doctor, Grandmother will be gone" | Children's anger speaks for the elders |
| Shige's House | 53–58 | Kurazo buys pastries deemed too expensive; Shige phones Noriko to take over | Outsourcing the parents |
| Noriko's Day | 59–73 | Sightseeing bus; borrowing sake and flask in apartment; Shoji's photo; delivery food | The only sincere hospitality |
| Atami | 74–91 | Siblings pool 3,000 yen; mahjong, buskers, sleepless night; "Place for the young"; Tomi sways | Filial duty = shipping them away; plant for illness |
| Homeless | 92–94 | Run into salon conference; "We've ended up homeless after all" (smile); peanuts in Ueno Park | Midpoint |
| Two Night Tracks | 95–108 | Father's drinking session: Numata on section chief vs. director, parents' selfish desires; mother at Noriko's: "Decided to stop aging"; drunken father returned to beauty parlor | Parallel montage |
| Leaving Tokyo | 109–112 | Noriko gives pocket money; "No need to rush back if anything happens" | Death omen cut short with a smile |

### Example A: Homeless (Scenes 93–94)
```
Shukichi: It wouldn't do to go back and trouble Koichi again...
Tomi: True enough—how about spending the night at Noriko's?
Shukichi: No, there isn't room for the two of us there. You go stay with her yourself...
Tomi: And what about you?
Shukichi: I thought I'd drop in on Hattori; surely he can put me up for the night. Well, let's be off.
Tomi: All right.
Shukichi: (Smiling) We've ended up homeless after all...
Tomi smiles and nods.
...
Shukichi: My, Tokyo really is enormous.
Tomi: Indeed. If you were to lose your way here, you might never be found again.
```

### Example B: Father Speaking Truth to an Outsider (Scene 101)
```

Just as you say, I felt let down too. But when you think about it, Numata, that is just parents' selfish desires, and parents' greed has no limit. So you simply have to make peace with it. Numata: So you too...

```
Tomi: But even if things are fine now, as you get older, living all alone will become so lonely.

Tomi: (Tearfully moved) What a good soul you are...
Noriko: (Softly) Let's go to sleep.

```

```

### Example F: Shige's Pragmatism (Scene 157)
```
Shige: Still, how should I put it? It sounds bad to say, but if one of them had to go first, it would have been better if Father had gone first... I mean, better that Father went before Mother.
Koichi: Hmm.
Shige: As things stand, once Kyoko marries, Father will have a hard time alone.
Koichi: That's true.
Shige: If it were Mother, we could bring her to Tokyo and manage easily enough. Oh, Kyoko, didn't Mother have a summer obi? Mouse-gray, with that dew-on-grass pattern...

Shige: I'd like to take that as a keepsake, if that's all right. Brother—
```

### Example G: Concluding Thematic Dialogue (Scene 162)
```
Kyoko: Mother has only just died, and already they're clamoring for keepsakes. Thinking of how Mother would feel breaks my heart. Even total strangers would show more warmth. I don't believe parents and children should be like this.
Noriko: But Kyoko, when I was your age, I used to think the very same thing. Once children grow up, though, they inevitably drift away from their parents. ... Everyone comes to put their own life first.
Kyoko: Really? But I don't want to become like that. If that's how it is, family is so cold and meaningless.
Noriko: It is. But isn't that how everyone ends up? Bit by bit, that's what happens.
Kyoko: Will you change like that too, Noriko?
Noriko: Yes. I don't want to, but I'll probably turn out just the same.
Kyoko: What an awful world this is...
Noriko: Yes. Life is full of disappointing things...
```

### Example H: The Watch (Scene 164)
```
Noriko: I really am selfish. I could never bring myself to tell Mother any of this.
Shukichi: Don't worry about it. It's fine as it is. You are a truly good person, and so honest...
Noriko: I don't deserve such praise.
Shukichi: No, you do...
Saying this, he stands up and brings a woman's wristwatch from a drawer beneath the Buddhist altar.
Shukichi: This was your mother's watch. It's quite out of fashion now, but she began wearing it right around your age. Please take it as a keepsake.
...
Shukichi: Strange, isn't it? Our own flesh and blood did far less for us than you, who are technically a stranger... Thank you.
With that, he bows his head in sorrow.
```

## IV. Floating Weeds (1959, 121 Scenes)

**Logline**: A traveling Kabuki troupe arrives at a southern fishing port. Troupe leader Komajuro (58) secretly visits his illegitimate son Kiyoshi (21), raised by noodle-shop owner Oyoshi to believe Komajuro is merely an uncle. His mistress Sumiko (34) uncovers the secret and pays young actress Kayo to seduce Kiyoshi; when the troupe goes bankrupt, Kiyoshi returns from an attempted elopement, Oyoshi reveals the truth, and Kiyoshi rejects him. Komajuro sets off alone, only to reunite with Sumiko at the train station.

| Sequence | Scene No. | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–8 | Lighthouse, waiting room gossip, exhausted troupe in boat cabin | Troupe introduced by strangers |
| Street Parade | 9–17 | Street ballyhoo; comics each set sights on a local woman | Laying subplots |
| Backstage | 18–22 | Theater manager delivers sake; introduces Sumiko, Kayo | Character roll call |
| Secret | 23–29 | Veering into Tsuruya; twelve-year reunion with Oyoshi; fishing date | Secret revealed to audience |
| Opening Night | 30–40 | Kunisada Chuji; peeking at house from behind curtain | Play within a play |
| Subplot 1 | 41–43 | Sentaro feels chill; Yatazo forced into shave | Comic relief |
| Father and Son | 44 | Fishing on pier: "Marubashi Chuya has no social relevance" | Son unaware he speaks to father |
| Suspicion | 45–48 | Sighted in barbershop mirror; Sumiko interrogates | Spotted through a mirror |

## V. Late Autumn (1960, 110 Scenes)

**Logline**: Three old classmates (Mamiya, Taguchi, Hirayama) gather on the seventh anniversary of their friend's death and resolve to arrange a match for his daughter Ayako (24); Ayako refuses to marry because she cannot abandon her mother. The trio concocts a plan to marry off the mother, Akiko (45), first, putting forward the widowed Hirayama as candidate; word reaches Ayako, provoking a bitter breach between mother and daughter. ## VI. An Autumn Afternoon (1962, 98 Scenes, Ozu's Final Film)

**Logline**: Widower Shuhei Hirayama (57) lives with his daughter Michiko (24) and younger son Kazuo. Old classmate Kawai urges him to marry Michiko off; at an alumni dinner, Hirayama reunites with their old teacher, the Gourd, and sees him eking out a grim existence running a dingy noodle stall, having kept his daughter unwed out of selfish convenience. Shaken, Hirayama resolves to marry Michiko off; when it turns out Miura, the man she likes, is already engaged, Michiko accepts an arranged marriage. On the wedding night, Hirayama sits alone at a bar listening to the Warship March, then returns home humming "All alone in the world."
