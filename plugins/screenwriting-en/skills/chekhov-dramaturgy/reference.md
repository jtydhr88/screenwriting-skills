> Use alongside SKILL.md. Translations: Jiao Juyin (Ivanov, The Seagull, Uncle Vanya, Three Sisters, The Cherry Orchard), Tong Daoming (Without Fathers, The Wood Demon), Li Jianwu (One-Act Plays).

## I. *Without Fathers* (*Platonov*, c. 1880, Four Acts)

**Logline**: General's widow Anna opens her estate to guests for the summer. Village schoolmaster Platonov (27, formerly a "second Byron", now a "stone lying flat") finds himself surrounded by four women in a single night: his wife Sasha; Anna (a nocturnal visit on horseback: "let us go"); former lover Sofya (now married to Anna's stepson, sending a telegraphic note: "come take me away"); and Grekova, a young woman he publicly humiliated. He goes to Sofya; Sasha attempts suicide by lying on the tracks, then by swallowing match heads; Sofya reveals the truth to her husband; the horse thief Osip is hired to kill him; the estate is auctioned off; Platonov points a pistol at his temple, then lowers it saying "I want to live"; Grekova arrives to take him away; Sofya enters and shoots him, the second bullet pressed directly against his chest. Final line: "Give him three rubles!"

| Act | Scene / Ambient Sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I (22 scenes) | Drawing room, piano, violin, harmonium | Playing chess and discussing Grekova; Old Glagolyev indulges in nostalgia; Platonov and wife arrive; on deceased father: "either speak the truth, or say nothing at all"; humiliates Grekova with "bedbugs and ether" then apologizes; Sofya recognizes him; false teeth; "three paths"; Young Vengerovich refuses to shake hands; Osip displayed as "a bloodthirsty beast from a modern zoological museum"; Anna: "either the estate or conscience... I choose the estate" | A single reception day: every character enters in sequence, offended one by one by the protagonist |
| II, Sc. 1 (21 scenes) | Nocturnal garden banquet; skittles game: "Five good ones! Four bad ones!"; fireworks | Borrowing money, scattering money; Colonel's drunken talk; Sofya: "Let us leave this place"; "You have lost your nerve"; hiring Osip for "a beating"; Grekova kissed and insulted; "The friendship between a man and a woman is only one step away from love"; "I have heaven, but no angel"; son deceives father: "She wants a thousand rubles" -> stroke; "Why cannot we live as we ought to live?!"; Sofya: "I bless you, new life!" -> fireworks | All four plotlines ignited within a single night |
| II, Sc. 2 (17 scenes) | In front of the school, railway embankment, telegraph poles; night; train | Osip recounts falling in love with Anna; "Why are you not a fly"; nocturnal conversation with Young Vengerovich; **Anna arrives on horseback: "Let us go!"**; Katya delivers letter: "Come take me away. Yours, me"; drunken Voynitsev: "I play Hamlet, you play Claudius"; Osip fires a shot into the air; **Sasha lies across the railway tracks -> lifted away -> train passes** | Three women's summons alternate across the very same stretch of embankment |
| III (10 scenes) | Schoolroom, clutter, wine bottles | Three weeks later. Sofya: "Tomorrow we leave"; reading Anna's letter; court summons -> writes letter to Grekova across the summons; Anna arrives to drink: "Live like a human being, you fool"; he shuts the door and plugs his ears; **Osip arrives to kill him**: tears up the banknotes: "So you won't think I am killing you for money"; Sasha: "Is the affair over?" -> leaves; "Give her back to me"; Old Glagolyev: "Is she an honest woman?" -> faints -> "Let us go to Paris" | Protagonist tried in turn by five separate visitors inside a single room |
| IV (13 scenes) | General's study; walls lined with firearms and swords; morning; rain | "I am a cuckolded husband" kneeling in plea; Osip beaten to death by peasants; estate bought up; Grekova drops the lawsuit; Platonov with bandaged hand: "Do not commit suicide... I have killed myself"; "Give me something hot"; "Committed suicide by swallowing match heads!" "She isn't dead?" "Alive!" (loud laughter); Colonel: "Lies can sometimes save a person too"; aimed at temple: "I want to live"; "Where does it hurt?" "Platonov is hurting"; Sofya fires; "Give him three rubles!" | All characters complete their reckoning inside the same study; death played as comedy |

**Example: Osip Arrives to Kill (Act III, Scene 7)**
```
OSIP. Do you want to know?
PLATONOV. Come on! How amusing! Where are we going?
OSIP. To the other world.
PLATONOV. That's a long way! (Pause) Could it be that you are the one sending me there?
OSIP. Exactly so. I have brought your passport.
...
OSIP. (Takes out some banknotes) Look... Vengerovich gave me these. He asked me to finish you off! (Tears up the banknotes)
PLATONOV. Hmm... Why did you tear up the money? Trying to show off your magnanimity, are you?
OSIP. I don't show off magnanimity. I tore up the money so you wouldn't think I am killing you for money.
```

**Example: Ending (Act IV)**
```
[Sofya picks up the pistol and fires at Platonov, missing.
GREKOVA. (Standing between Platonov and Sofya) What are you doing?! (Shouting) Somebody help! Quick, somebody come!
SOFYA. Let go...
[Stepping around Grekova, she presses the muzzle against Platonov's chest and fires.
PLATONOV. Wait a moment, wait a moment... Why should it be like this? (Collapses)
...
MARKO. (Enters) The conciliation court... (Pause)
ANNA. Platonov!
[Platonov sits up slightly, sweeping his eyes over everyone.
ANNA. Platonov... it is nothing... drink some water!
PLATONOV. (Pointing at Marko) Give him three rubles! (Lies back down, dies)
...
TRILETSKY. Life is worth a kopeck! Misha, farewell! Your kopeck is gone!
```

## II. *Ivanov* (1887, Four-Act Drama)

**Logline**: Thirty-five-year-old landowner Ivanov, once full of fervor, ideals, and devotion when he married Jewish woman Anna (who converted for him and was disowned by her parents), has grown weary of everything, fleeing every evening to his neighbor Lebedev's home; the neighbor's twenty-year-old daughter Sasha seeks to save him through "active love"; his wife catches the two embracing, driving him to lose control and blurt out "You are going to die soon"; a year later, his wife dead, he catches sight of his gray hair in the mirror before his wedding to Sasha, demands to call off the engagement, is publicly denounced as a scoundrel by Dr. Lvov, and draws a pistol to kill himself.

| Act | Scene / Ambient Sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Estate garden; duet of piano and cello; owl; night watchman; accordion in the distance | Borkin scares him with a gun, asks for eighty-two rubles; doctor announces consumption; Ivanov confesses: "There is nothing extraordinary about me"; advises doctor: "Marry someone ordinary and dull"; Lvov's monologue: "I hate this Tartuffe"; Anna begs him to stay, he replies: "Pray for me" | Unpacks every character's sickness within a single act: weariness, consumption, miserliness, misanthropy, moral fanaticism |
| II | Drawing room at the Lebedevs'; card game calls: "Pass", "Two of spades"; old women's idle gossip | Sasha's birthday; three versions of the rumor (garlic, cattle plague, nine thousand rubles); Sasha scolds the young men: "You are all good-for-nothings"; Dzyuzyushka blows out candles; plea for interest deferral refused; Borkin proposes to Babakina on the Count's behalf; **Sasha confesses -> "Life begins anew" -> Anna freezes in the doorway -> "Sarah!" Curtain** | Rumor scene + miser scene + proposal farce + caught at curtain |
| III | Study; desk strewn with documents, pistol, vodka, herring, cucumber; noon | Discussing French and German politics over cucumbers and carp; Lebedev offers eleven thousand then takes it back: "I was only joking just now"; "I have strained myself into a hernia"; "I hate my own voice, footsteps, hands"; Lvov: "Take off your mask"; Sasha: "Have you ever forged counterfeit banknotes? ... Smash something"; **Anna presses him -> "Shut up, you Jewish woman! ... You are going to die soon" -> "When did he say that?"** | Protagonist unburdens himself twice; the cruelest line delivered at the act's end |
| IV | One year later; wedding atmosphere; piano, violin, cello | Lvov: "I will expose you"; Kosykh still talking cards; Sasha: "Sometimes I even feel I do not love him as I ought to love him"; Count weeps for late wife upon seeing cello; "I looked in the mirror and saw the gray hair at my temples... let it end right here"; "The ceiling is white, boots are black, sugar is sweet"; "You are a scoundrel!" - "I am very grateful to you"; "This is not a wedding, it is a debate"; "My youth has awakened in my heart" -> fires shot | Wedding turned into debate; suicide as an ironic revival of the "former self" |

**Example: Act III Curtain**
```
IVANOV. Hmph, let me tell you then, you... you are going to die soon... The doctor told me, he said you are going to die soon...
ANNA. (Sits down, in a low voice) When did he say that?
[Pause.
IVANOV. (Clutching his head with both hands) I am nothing but a beast! My God, nothing but a beast! (Sobs)
-- Curtain falls
```

**Example: Lebedev's Common Sense (Act IV)**
```
Cool your head down! Look at things simply, like everyone else! Everything in this world is simple. The ceiling is white, boots are black, sugar is sweet. You love Sasha, and she loves you. If you love her, you stay; if you don't love her, you leave; there is no need for us to make a mountain out of a molehill.
```

## III. *The Seagull* (1896, Four-Act Comedy)

**Logline**: An estate by a lake. Young Treplyov stages his "new forms" play on a homemade open-air stage, starring the girl he loves, Nina; his mother, the actress Arkadina, mocks it, and he furiously rings down the curtain. Nina falls under the spell of his mother's lover, the writer Trigorin. Treplyov shoots a seagull and lays it at Nina's feet, then attempts suicide. Trigorin elopes with Nina, then abandons her. Two years later, Nina returns for one night as a third-rate actress, recites her monologue from back then, and leaves; Treplyov tears up his manuscripts, an offstage gunshot rings out, and the doctor says: "A bottle of ether in my medicine chest just exploded."

**Chain of Love (entirely unidirectional)**: Medvedenko -> Masha -> Treplyov -> Nina -> Trigorin <-> Arkadina; Polina -> Dorn; Sorin wished to marry and become a writer, accomplishing neither ("L'homme qui a voulu").

| Act | Scene / Ambient Sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Garden, amateur stage blocking the lake; hammering; dogs howling all night; stage trumpet, smell of sulfur, will-o'-the-wisps | "Why do you always wear black?" "I am in mourning for my life"; play-within-a-play: "Men, lions, eagles, and partridges..." interrupted by mother with "decadent", "sulfur smell" -> "Curtain!"; "An angel flew past"; Dorn alone praises the play; Masha leans on Dorn's chest: "I love Konstantin" | The very first line establishes the theme; the play-within-a-play is the embryo of the entire drama; act ends transformed into confession |
| II | Croquet lawn, midday, reflection of lake water | Arkadina compares youth with Masha; estate manager argues over horses: "Then I resign"; Polina begs Dorn to take her away, destroys flowers; **Dead seagull placed at Nina's feet: "Soon I shall kill myself in the same way"**; Trigorin's long monologue (clouds like grand pianos, "inferior to Turgenev"); jotting notes: "On the shore of a lake... lives a girl like a seagull... a man comes along by chance... destroys her" | All three symbolic layers of the seagull planted; notebook subject = prophecy |
| III | Dining room, luggage trunks | Masha: "I am getting married"; medallion: "Page 121, lines 11 and 12"; mother and son change bandages -> quarrel -> reconcile; "If you ever need my life, come and take it" -> kneeling to clasp knees: "You are Russia's only hope" -> (aside) "Now I have caught him"; manager tells of the "depraved rooster"; returns for walking stick, hotel rendezvous, long kiss | Three scenes of "holding on" within a single act |
| IV | Two years later; drawing room turned study; wind howling; night watchman's rattle; waltz; calling lotto numbers | Stage "like a dead skeleton"; literary journal uncut at his story; Dorn on "crowds of Genoa" -> "Where is Zarechnaya"; stuffed seagull: "I don't remember"; **Nina arrives: "I am a seagull... no, that's not it", "One must have patience, know how to bear one's cross, have faith", recites "Men, lions, eagles, and partridges" again, runs out**; tearing manuscripts; card game continues; **Gunshot -> "A bottle of ether just exploded" -> "Find some way to take Irina Nikolayevna away"** | All characters gather around the card table; protagonist's death handled through an offstage lie |

**Example: Opening**
```
MEDVEDENKO. Why do you always wear black?
MASHA. I am in mourning for my life. I am unhappy.
MEDVEDENKO. Why is that? (Silence) I don't understand... Your health is good, and though your father isn't wealthy, he is comfortable enough. My life is far harder than yours. I make only twenty-three rubles a month, with pension deductions on top of that. Yet even under such conditions, I don't wear mourning.
```

**Example: Holding On (Act III)**
```
TRIGORIN. I have no will of my own... I have never had a will of my own... Flaccid, weak, forever submissive, was I truly born to disgust women! So take me, carry me away, only, I beg you, never let me leave your side for a single step...
ARKADINA. (Aside) Now I have caught him. (Calmly, as if nothing had just occurred) Well, if you wish, you may stay. I shall leave today, and you can join me in a week. Speaking of which, why must you be in such a hurry?
TRIGORIN. No, it is better that we leave together.
```

**Example: Ending**
```
SHAMRAYEV. (Leading Trigorin toward the cupboard) That thing I mentioned to you just now is right here... (Takes the stuffed seagull from the cupboard) This is what you ordered us to make.
TRIGORIN. (Gazing at the seagull) I don't remember! (Reflecting) No, I don't remember!
[Offstage to the right, a gunshot rings out; everyone jumps in alarm.
ARKADINA. (Frightened) What was that?
DORN. Nothing. Something in my medicine chest must have popped. Don't be alarmed. (Exits through right door, returns immediately) Exactly as I said. A bottle of ether in my medicine chest just exploded. (Hums softly) "Once again I stand before you, enchanting woman..."
ARKADINA. (Sitting down by the card table) How it terrified me! It reminded me of that time when he... (Covers her face with both hands) That sight made everything go black before my eyes.
DORN. (Flipping through a magazine, to Trigorin) About two months ago, an article was published in this magazine... a letter from America; regarding this, I wanted to ask you... (Puts his arm around Trigorin's waist, pulling him toward the footlights) This matter is exceedingly interesting... (In a whisper) Find some way to take Irina Nikolayevna away. Konstantin Gavrilovich has just shot himself...
-- Curtain falls
```

## IV. *The Wood Demon* (1889, Four-Act Comedy) -> *Uncle Vanya* (1896, "Scenes from Country Life")

***Uncle Vanya* Logline**: Retired Professor Serebryakov brings his young wife Yelena to live on his late wife's estate, upending the twenty-five-year routine of Uncle Vanya and niece Sonya, who labored to support him; Vanya is infatuated with Yelena, and Dr. Astrov is likewise captivated by her, neglecting both forests and patients; Sonya harbors a hopeless six-year love for the doctor; the professor announces his plan to sell the estate, causing Vanya to erupt and fire two shots at him, missing both times; the professor and his wife leave, the doctor leaves, and Vanya and Sonya return to their ledger: "We shall rest."

| Act | Scene / Ambient Sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Garden, tea table under old poplar, garden swing, guitar; overcast afternoon around three o'clock | "How long have we known each other?" "Eleven years"; "The professor will stay until doomsday"; "Famous nonentity" speech; Telyegin's wife ran away; "Fine weather to hang oneself"; chick-chick; forest monologue (Sonya recounts it first, then doctor delivers it, cut off by vodka: "In short..."); Yelena: "Every one of you has a destructive nature"; Vanya's courtship: "Do not drive me away" | Every character's "sickness" laid out around the tea table |
| II | Dining room, night, night watchman's rattle; thunderstorm | Professor keeping night watch: "I dreamed my left leg fell off", "In five or six years I shall be old too"; Vanya drunk: "Ten years ago she was seventeen and I was thirty-seven", "Soap bubble"; doctor drunk singing: "Acquaintance -> mistress -> friend"; Sonya and doctor eating dried cheese: lights in the forest, "I love no one", "If I had a sister" -> "I would only let her understand that I cannot love her"; "Why am I not beautiful"; drinking from the same cup to make peace; "I want to play the piano" -> watchman halted -> "He won't allow it" | Every pair of characters attempts closeness once, and each fails |
| III | Drawing room, three doors; afternoon | "Idleness and indolence are contagious"; Vanya goes to fetch autumn roses; Sonya confesses: "I have loved him for six years"; Yelena: "I shall talk to him" - Sonya at the door: "No, uncertainty is better after all"; **Three maps** -> "You take no interest in this at all"; "Do you like her as a woman?" "No"; "A beautiful, fur-soft predatory cat"; kiss -> **Vanya standing in the doorway holding the roses** -> "What fine weather we are having today... only the days are growing shorter"; family meeting ("The Government Inspector", manet omnes una nox, sell estate to buy Finnish dacha); "Twenty-five years like a mole"; Nurse: "Let the turkeys gobble and fight"; **Offstage gunshots -> "Ah, bang! Missed? Missed again?!"** | Three maps = charts of degeneration; bouquet of roses serves as the cruelest prop |
| IV | Vanya's bedroom and office; ledgers, scale, starling, map of Africa; autumn night | Winding yarn: "Better this way"; pistol hidden in cellar; doctor asks for morphine; "Both of us have ruined our lives past redemption"; Yelena takes a pencil: "Whatever happens! Just this once in a lifetime!" a kiss; "Finita la comedia"; Professor: "One must achieve something"; map of Africa: "Must still be dreadfully hot there?"; Nurse brings vodka; harness bells; "February 2nd, oil, twenty pounds..."; Sonya's monologue; watchman, guitar, marginal notes, yarn | Everything returns to the order that predated Act I |

**Comparison between *The Wood Demon* and *Uncle Vanya* can be found in Section VII of SKILL.md. Original Act II ending of *The Wood Demon* (preserved as is):**
```
YELENA. It has been so long since I played. I want to play and weep, weeping like a silly fool. (Looks out the window) Yefim, is that you on watch?
WATCHMAN'S VOICE. It is me!
YELENA. Stop beating the board, the master is unwell.
WATCHMAN'S VOICE. Leaving right away! (Whistling) Hey there, little dog Zhuchka! Zhuchka!
[Silence on stage.
SONYA. (Returns) He won't allow it!
-- Curtain falls
```

**Example: Caught in the Act (Act III)**
```
ASTROV. (Clasping her waist, holding her) Come to the forester's lodge tomorrow... around two o'clock. You will come, won't you?
YELENA. (Sees Voynitsky) Let go of me! (Extremely flustered, walks to the window) This is terrible.
VOYNITSKY. (Places the bouquet of flowers on a chair, trembling with emotion, wiping sweat from his face and neck with a handkerchief) It doesn't matter... no... it doesn't matter at all...
ASTROV. (With a displeased expression) My dear Ivan Petrovich, what fine weather we are having today. It was rather overcast this morning, looking like rain, but now look, what sunshine. To tell the truth, this autumn has been remarkably fine... and the harvest isn't bad either. (Rolls up the map) Only the days are growing shorter... (Exits)
```

**Example: The Gunshots (Act III Curtain)**
```
VOYNITSKY. Let go of me, Yelena, let go of me! (Tears himself away from her, rushes upstage, searching with his eyes for Serebryakov) Where did he run off to? Ha, there he is! (Fires) Ah, bang!
[Pause.
    Missed? Missed again?! (Furious) Ah, you... to hell with you... (Tosses the pistol carelessly to the floor, collapsing into a chair in utter exhaustion...)
YELENA. Take me away! Take me away, kill me, but... I cannot stay here a moment longer!
VOYNITSKY. (In despair) Ah, what have I done! What on earth have I done!
SONYA. (Softly) Old Mother! Old Mother!
```

**Example: Ending (Act IV)**
```
VOYNITSKY. (Writing) "February 2nd, oil, twenty pounds... February 16th, another twenty pounds of oil dispatched... buckwheat..."
[Pause. Harness bells ring out.
MARINA. He has gone.
[Pause.
SONYA. (Returns, sets the candle back on the table) Gone...
VOYNITSKY. (Clicking the abacus, then writing down the sum) Totals... fifteen... twenty-five...
...
SONYA. What can we do, we must go on living!
[Pause.
    We shall go on living, Uncle Vanya, we have a long, long string of monotonous days and nights ahead of us... We shall work for others right into our old age... and we shall see a bright, joyful, beautiful life... (Involuntarily kneels before him, resting her face upon his hands, in a low voice) We shall rest at last!
[Telyegin gently strums the guitar.
    We shall rest! We shall hear the angels, and see the whole sky studded with diamonds...
[The night watchman's rattle sounds in the distance.
[Telyegin plays softly. Marya Vasilyevna writes notes in the margins of her pamphlet. Marina knits wool.
    Ah, rest!
-- The curtain falls slowly
```

## V. *Three Sisters* (1900, Four-Act Drama)

**Logline**: In a provincial town, the three daughters of a general—Olga (schoolteacher), Masha (married to a high school teacher), and Irina (twenty)—along with their brother Andrey, dream of returning to Moscow; the officers of the artillery battery are their only conversational companions. Andrey marries the petty-bourgeois Natasha, who takes over the house room by room, expels the old nurse, and carries on an affair with the council chairman; Andrey loses money gambling and mortgages the house; Masha falls in love with Lieutenant Colonel Vershinin, who has a mad wife; Irina agrees to marry Baron Tuzenbach, whom she does not love, and go to work at a brickworks; on the day the brigade departs, Solyony kills the baron in a duel; as the military march fades, the three sisters lean on one another: "We must live."

Time: Act I, May 5th noon (first anniversary of father's death, Irina's name day); Act II, February the following year, Shrovetide evening at eight; Act III, summer night after two o'clock another year later during the fire; Act IV, autumn noon during the regiment's departure.

| Act | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|
| I | "Father died exactly one year ago today"; three sisters in three colors; "To Moscow"; Irina: "One must work"; Chebutykin reads paper: "Remedy for hair loss", brings silver samovar; Masha recites the green oak, prepares to leave, then stays; Vershinin: "People will forget us", "In two or three hundred years"; "Your clock is seven minutes fast"; thirteen at the table; Natasha's green sash; **Andrey proposes, walked in on by two officers** | Opening states the theme; "two or three hundred years from now" philosophy stated in full in Act I |
| II | Natasha inspects for fire hazards with a candle, demands Irina's room; Andrey pours his heart out to the deaf man; Ferapont: "Died from eating pancakes", "A rope stretched all across Moscow"; "Tarara-boom-de-ay"; debate on "two or three hundred years" (migrating cranes); "Balzac was married in Berdichev" x3; card divination: "You won't get to Moscow"; Vershinin gets letter: "My wife took poison again", slips away; argument over chekhartma vs. cheremsha; Solyony: "I shall kill my rival"; Natasha rides off in carriage with Protopopov; Irina alone: "To Moscow!" | Everyone's longing crushed beneath trivialities |
| III | Fire alarm bell; red glow. Anfisa: "Do not throw me out"; Natasha: "I forbid you to sit in my presence!"; Kulygin hides behind wardrobe; Chebutykin washes hands, monologue: "Perhaps I do not exist", **smashes mother's clock** "Shattered!", reveals affair; Fedotik dances: "Everything burned up!"; Kulygin: "I am satisfied" x3; "He mortgaged this house to the bank"; Irina breaks down: "I've even forgotten the Italian for window"; **Masha confesses - Olga hides behind screen: "I won't listen anyway"**; "I will marry him... only we must go to Moscow!" | Night of the fire = night of confession for the whole family |
| IV | Decaying garden, long avenue ending at river and forest; "Halloo! Oo-oo!". Lieutenants bid farewell: "Farewell, echo!"; Chebutykin: "Everyone forgot to say goodbye to me"; Kulygin shaves his mustache; "What happened yesterday by the theater?" - "A trifle"; "Did you love my mother?" "Very much" "That I don't remember"; "What does it matter, one baron more or less"; Solyony sprinkles perfume: "Smells of a corpse"; Andrey to deaf man: "A hundred thousand inhabitants knowing only how to eat, drink, and sleep"; **Tuzenbach bids farewell: "That tree is dead, yet it sways in the wind with the others", "I haven't had any coffee today"**; Anfisa: "There is no one happier on earth than me"; long kiss; Kulygin: "Not a single word shall I say"; Masha misquotes: "A cat, green leaves all around"; **Gunshot in the distance**; false mustache; Natasha: "Why is this fork thrown on the ground"; military march; whispered: "The Baron was just killed in the duel" -> "I suspected it all along"; sisters cling together; "It doesn't matter, it doesn't matter" | Triple farewell layered across a single noon |

**Example: Andrey and the Deaf Man (Act II)**
```
ANDREY. ...I, who dreamed every night of becoming a professor at Moscow University, a celebrated scholar whom all Russia would take pride in, am nothing more than a member of the rural district council!
FERAPONT. I couldn't say... I didn't hear clearly...
ANDREY. If you could hear clearly, I probably wouldn't be talking to you. I have a great need to talk to someone.
...
FERAPONT. In Moscow, now... the other day a contractor was saying at the council office... he said in Moscow some merchants were eating pancakes; well now, it seems one fellow ate forty of them and died on the spot. Whether it was forty or fifty, I can't rightly remember.
```

**Example: The Smashed Clock (Act III)**
```
CHEBUTYKIN. (Drops the clock on the floor, smashing it to pieces) Shattered!
[Pause. Everyone looks distressed; all are in disarray.
KULYGIN. (Picking up the pieces) To smash such a valuable thing, look at you, Ivan Romanovich, Ivan Romanovich! Zero marks for conduct!
IRINA. That was Mother's clock.
CHEBUTYKIN. Perhaps... If it was Mother's, then Mother's it was. Perhaps I didn't smash it at all, only imagined I smashed it? Perhaps we only imagine that we exist, when in fact we don't exist at all? I don't know a thing, and nobody knows a thing. (Walks to the door) Why are you staring at me? You are all blind! Natasha is carrying on a nice little affair with Protopopov, and you see nothing at all...
```

**Example: Tuzenbach's Farewell (Act IV)**
```
TUZENBACH. ...I must go, it is time... You see that tree, it is dead; yet it still sways in the wind with the others. And so it seems to me that if I die, I shall still participate in life, in one way or another. Farewell, my darling... (Kisses her hands) Those papers you gave me are on my desk, tucked under the calendar.
IRINA. I am coming with you.
TUZENBACH. (Alarmed) No, no! (Walks away quickly, stops on the garden path) Irina!
IRINA. What is it?
TUZENBACH. (Not knowing what to say) I haven't had any coffee today. Tell them to make some for me, will you? (Exits in great haste)
```

**Example: Gunshot and Ending (Act IV)**
```
MASHA. (Choking back her sobs) "By the seashore stands a green oak... upon the tree hangs a chain of gold, shining bright..." A golden chain upon the tree... I am losing my mind... By the seashore... a green oak...
OLGA. Calm yourself, Masha... calm yourself... give her some water.
MASHA. I'm not crying anymore...
KULYGIN. She's not crying anymore... she is so good...
[A distant, muffled gunshot rings out.
MASHA. By the seashore stands a green oak, upon the tree hangs a chain of gold... a cat, green leaves all around... an oak tree, green leaves all around... I've got it all mixed up...
...
CHEBUTYKIN. The Baron was just killed in the duel.
IRINA. (Weeping silently) I suspected it all along, I suspected it all along...
CHEBUTYKIN. (Walks to the far background, sitting on a bench) I am worn out to death... (Pulls the newspaper from his pocket) Let her weep... (Hums softly) "Tell us, what can you do? Speak up, will you play the good-for-nothing?..." It's all the same anyway!
[The three sisters stand together, holding each other tightly.
MASHA. Ah! Listen to that military music! They are leaving us, and one of them is gone forever, never to return, and we must begin our lives all over again, all alone... We must live... we must live...
...
OLGA. (Embracing her two sisters) What cheerful, lively music, it makes one so long to live!... We must live! How joyful, how merry the music is! It makes one feel as if in just a little while we shall know why we live, why we suffer... If only we could know! Ah, if only we could know!
[The music fades into the distance. Kulygin, smiling happily, brings out Masha's hat and cape; Andrey pushes the carriage with the baby inside.
CHEBUTYKIN. (Hums softly) "Tell us, tell us, what can you do? Speak up, will you play the good-for-nothing?..." (Looks at his newspaper) It doesn't matter, it doesn't matter.
OLGA. If only we could know, if only we could know!
-- Curtain falls
```

## VI. *The Cherry Orchard* (1903, Four-Act Comedy)

**Logline**: Landowner Lyubov returns from Paris to her ancestral cherry orchard, which is about to be sold at auction; Lopakhin, the son of a serf and now a wealthy merchant, repeatedly urges her to parcel out the land for summer dachas, but neither she nor her brother Gayev can bring themselves to listen; on the day of the auction, a ball is held at the house, and Lopakhin buys the cherry orchard; in October the family scatters, Trofimov the "eternal student" and daughter Anya set off toward a "new life", Lyubov returns to Paris to the lover who deceived her, eighty-seven-year-old manservant Firs is left locked inside the empty house, and the sound of an axe striking a tree echoes in the distance.

| Act | Time / Setting / Sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | May dawn, "nursery", windows closed, morning frost in orchard, cherries in bloom | Lopakhin falls asleep waiting for train; memories of being a "little peasant"; Yepikhodov's squeaking boots, drops flowers, bumps into chairs: "Look at that, won't you!"; everyone arrives: "The nursery!"; "The auction is in August"; Lopakhin's plan: divide into parcels for dachas, twenty-five thousand; "Cut down the trees of the cherry orchard!"; recipe for dried cherries "forgotten"; tearing Paris telegram; **Gayev's ode to the century-old bookcase**; Pishchik swallows all the pills: "Lend me two hundred and forty rubles"; window opens: "The whole orchard is all white"; "Mother is walking through the garden" - a small leaning white poplar; Trofimov arrives: "Grisha, my little boy!", "How ugly you've grown, how old"; shepherd's pipe | Reunion day: the solution is presented and rejected in the very first act |
| II | Open country, abandoned chapel, well, gravestones, telegraph poles, distant town silhouette; sunset | Charlotta: "I have no passport... who am I?" munching cucumber; Yepikhodov with pistol in pocket, "an extraordinarily large spider"; Yasha: "Pretending I've just had a dip in the river"; Lopakhin: "Just one word: yes or no"; "Dachas and summer visitors... how terribly vulgar"; Lyubov's confession (husband died of champagne, lover, son drowned, attempted poison) -> tears second telegram -> "Do I hear music in the distance?"; "Why don't you marry Varya?" "Indeed! Who says no?"; Firs: "Before the emancipation of the serfs"; Trofimov's speech; **Sound of a breaking string**: "A cable snapped in a mine", "Heron", "Owl", "Just like this before the great misfortune"; passerby quotes Nadson, asks for thirty kopecks, gets gold ruble; "Okhmelia, get thee to a nunnery"; "All Russia is our orchard", "Throw the keys into the well"; moon rises | The only outdoor act, the only sunset; prophecy appears here |
| III | August 22nd evening, drawing room and ballroom, Jewish band, quadrille | Pishchik: "Descended from Caligula's horse", money slips through pocket lining; Varya weeping while dancing; Charlotta's magic tricks, ventriloquism, produces two girls from beneath a rug; "Madame Lopakhin!", "The eternal student!"; Yepikhodov breaks billiard cue; Lyubov begs Trofimov: "Talk to me" -> third telegram: "I love him, he is like a stone around my neck" -> "You haven't even grown a proper beard", "At your age not even to have a mistress" -> Petya falls down stairs -> dancing resumes; stationmaster recites *The Sinful Woman*, drowned out by waltz; Yasha: "I hope you close your eyes soon"; stranger in kitchen says "Sold"; Varya swings stick, hits Lopakhin by mistake: "Much obliged!"; Gayev: "Pickled whitefish and herring", hears billiard balls, stops weeping; **"Sold." Strike up, music!", "Why wouldn't you listen to me"; knocks over candelabra: "I can pay for everything"; Anya kneels: "We shall plant a new orchard" | Ball = counterpoint to auction; catastrophe announced by the buyer |
| IV | October, same as Act I, curtains and picture frames removed, furniture piled in corners | Champagne untouched, Yasha: "Not real champagne"; "Only forty-six minutes until the train"; looking for galoshes; "Two thousand desyatinas of poppies netted forty thousand"; "I am a free man... I walk in the front ranks"; **Offstage axe chopping trees**; "Was Firs sent to the hospital?" "Sent" (not sent); squashes hatbox: "Look at that, won't you!"; Yasha avoids seeing mother; Charlotta cradles bundle like a baby: "Find me another position"; Pishchik pays back money: "White clay", "Once there was a man named Semyonov-Pishchik"; **Proposal scene: "I have no thermometer" - "Yermolay!" - "Coming!"**; "Well?" pause "Well, let us go then!"; "Double into the middle pocket... I shall hold my tongue"; "I was only six... sat on this windowsill watching Father go to church"; Varya pulls umbrella, Lopakhin defends himself; "Farewell, old house!", "Welcome, new life!"; doors locked one by one, carriages drive off; **Firs**: "Locked. They've gone... They forgot all about me... Life has slipped by so fast... you good-for-nothing!"; breaking string; axe | Same setting as Act I, reverse trajectory |

**Example: The Bookcase Speech (Act I)**
```
GAYEV. ...Dear, highly respected bookcase! For over a hundred years you have directed your existence toward the high ideals of justice and happiness; ah, you! I salute you...
[Pause.
LOPAKHIN. Yes...
LYUBOV. You haven't changed a bit, Lyonya.
GAYEV. (A little embarrassed) White ball into the corner pocket, carom red into the middle!
```

**Example: The Breaking String (Act II)**
```
[Everyone sits motionless, each lost in their own thoughts, total silence. Only Firs can be heard muttering. Suddenly, far away, as if falling from the sky, comes a sound resembling a snapped string, dying away melancholy and faint.
LYUBOV. What was that?
LOPAKHIN. I don't know. Perhaps a bucket broke loose in some distant mine shaft. But very far away.
GAYEV. Perhaps some kind of bird... a heron or something.
TROFIMOV. Or an owl...
LYUBOV. (Shudders) There is something eerie about that sound!
[Pause.
FIRS. It was just like that before the great trouble; the owl hooted, and the samovar hummed without stopping.
GAYEV. Before what trouble?
FIRS. Before the Emancipation.
```

**Example: Sold (Act III)**
```
LYUBOV. Is the cherry orchard sold?
LOPAKHIN. Sold.
LYUBOV. Who bought it?
LOPAKHIN. I did.
[Pause.
[Lyubov Andreyevna feels faint; had she not held onto a nearby table and armchair, she would have fallen to the floor. Varya unhooks the ring of keys from her belt, flings them down onto the floor in the middle of the drawing room, and leaves.
    I bought it. Wait, please, ladies and gentlemen, don't rush, my head is spinning, I cannot speak...
...
    Hey! Musicians, play! I want to hear your music! Let everyone come and see me, come and see Yermolay Lopakhin lay the axe to this cherry orchard! Come see the trees crash down one by one!... Strike up, music!
[The band plays. Lyubov Andreyevna sinks into a chair, weeping bitterly.
    (In a reproachful tone) Why didn't you listen to me! My poor, kind Lyubov Andreyevna! It's too late now. (With tears in his eyes) Ah! If only all this could be ended, how good it would be!
```

**Example: The Proposal That Never Happened (Act IV)**
```
LOPAKHIN. This time last year it was already snowing, you may recall. But now, you see, the weather is bright and sunny everywhere. Just a bit chilly... three degrees of frost already.
VARYA. I have no thermometer.
[Pause.
    Besides, the thermometer is broken...
[Pause.
[Voices from the yard outside: "Yermolay Alexeyevich!"
LOPAKHIN. (As if he had long been waiting for just such a call) Coming! (Exits in great haste)
[Varya sits on the floor, resting her head upon a bundle of clothes, weeping softly. The door opens, and Lyubov Andreyevna enters cautiously.
LYUBOV. Well?
[Pause.
    Well, let us go then!
```

**Example: Ending**
```
[They all exit; the stage is empty. Only the sound of doors being locked one after another can be heard from outside, followed by the sound of carriages driving away. Complete silence. In this silence, the dull thud of an axe striking a tree rings out, forlorn and sorrowful.
[Footsteps are heard. Firs appears in the doorway to the right. He is still wearing his tailcoat and white waistcoat, but with slippers on his feet. He is ill.
FIRS. (Walks to the door on the left, turns the handle) Locked. They have gone... (Sits on the sofa) They forgot all about me... Never mind... I'll just sit here and wait. Leonid Andreyevich must have forgotten his fur coat, he'll have gone in his light overcoat for sure... (Sighs with concern) That's what happens when I'm not there to look after him!... Young greenhorn!... (Mutters something unintelligible) Life has slipped by so fast, as if I hadn't lived at all... (Lies down) I'll lie down... You haven't an ounce of strength left in you, not a thing! Everything is gone, all gone... Ah, you... you good-for-nothing!... (Lies motionless)
[Far away, as if falling from the sky, comes a sound resembling a snapped string, dying away melancholy and faint. Silence falls again. Breaking this silence, nothing is heard but the sound of an axe chopping trees far off in the orchard.
-- Curtain falls
```
