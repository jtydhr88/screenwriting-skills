# Dialogue: Seven Scene Beat Analysis Examples, Rewrite Comparisons, and Lyric Examples

> Used in conjunction with SKILL.md. Beat format: #Number "Line Summary" -- Action/Reaction (Gerund) + Subtext Essentials + Value Turn.

---

## I. Balanced Conflict: The Sopranos S5E1 Tony vs. Dr. Melfi

- Desire / Antagonism / Intent / Strategy: Tony wants Melfi's love; antagonism is that she cannot see his good side; scene intent is seduction; strategy is proving he is a good man. Melfi wants to treat him; antagonism is his attempt to turn the relationship personal; intent is therapeutic help; strategy is confronting him with facts. **Super-Intent**: Tony needs to control the only person who holds authority over him (a Romeo mask covering a lust for power); beneath Melfi's "opposed to danger" characterization lies a true persona that is "attracted to danger."
- Three Value Pairs: Doctor-patient relationship together / apart; Tony self-deception / self-awareness; Melfi mortal danger / safety.
- 13 Beats: #1 Exerting charm / bracing for trouble; #2 Handing Bermuda tickets "My friend cannot use them" -- Making a demand / laughing at his foolishness (she uses the rhetorical question "I turned down dinner, do you really think I would go with you?" to avoid direct conflict); #3 "What do I do, throw them out?" -- Professing innocence / avoiding conflict (she stays silent, "silence is sometimes more powerful than speech"); #4 "You make me feel like half a groupie" -- Bidding for sympathy / rebuking him; #5 "It cannot just be the therapist ethics thing" -- Seeking a graceful exit / offering him a ladder down (she instead offers the agonizing path of self-awareness); #6 "I want you!" -- Confessing / playing for time; #7 "Help me understand what is going on" -- Stirring trouble / crossing the line; #8 "Our values are very different" -- Backing him into a corner / scorning him; #9 "You do not like my values? -- Want the truth? -- (Beat) No" -- Challenging / negating his values; #10 "OK... For instance?" -- checking watch -- Inquiring gently / offering another graceful exit; #11 "No no no no no, it is not OK" -- "You are not honest. You do not respect women. You do not respect people." -- Demanding the worst / throwing a punch; #12 "I do not love people?" -- "You take whatever you want by brute force or threat" -- Self-doubting / firing the first shot; #13 "I cannot witness violence" -- "Fuck you... you fucking bitch" slamming the door -- Firing the second shot / murdering her with words (the opening established a dozen group-therapy patients who would serve as eyewitnesses, so he uses only the most toxic words).
- Content / Length / Rhythm Contrast: Tony speaks in monosyllables lacking complete sentences ("Tickets...", "Bermuda...", "Walk around"), half questions, the rest lies or pleas; Melfi uses three-to-four-syllable nouns in twenty-five-word complete grammatical sentences; Tony repeats "OK" five times like waves crashing against the shore, escalating. The one controlling rhythm wins.

## II. Comedic Conflict: Frasier S1 "Author, Author"

Frasier and Niles procrastinate until deadline day without writing a word. #1 "Niles, why do we not just admit we cannot work together?" -- "With that attitude, there will never be a book": Demanding acceptance of failure / shifting blame for failure; #2 "The fat soprano has already sung" -- "Mr. Big-Shot Radio Host": Calling him an idiot / calling him arrogant; #3 "Are you jealous of my fame?" -- "By the time I catch up, all that is left is chewed-up meat": Calling him an impossible-to-please child / calling him a scene-stealer (the incongruity of a mother chewing meat for a baby triggers laughter, resetting time back to infancy); #4 Calling him a masochist / calling him a sadist; #5 "I am the one on the psychiatric board... yet your big fat face is plastered on city buses": Calling him a crybaby / calling him a show-off (vulgarizing noble pursuits); #6 "I do not have a big fat face" -- "How much longer are you going to store those winter walnuts?": Defending his face / attacking his face (subtext surfaces into text); #7 "Spindle-face -- Fat-face -- Spindle-face -- Fat-face": Mocking looks / mocking looks; #8 Clenching fists / clenching fists; #9 "I will push you" -- "I do not see you pushing"; #10 Yanking chest hair; #11 Jumping onto his back with a headlock -- "We are psychologists, not brawlers": Attacking / coaxing (targeting vanity); #12 Surrendering / attacking; #13 "You climbed into my crib and jumped on me": Threatening murder / recalling childhood horror; #14 "You stole my mommy!" running away: Murdering his brother / fleeing the murder scene. Spiral: Personality -> Appearance -> Emotion -> Violence. Every beat lands on a punchline (Radio Big Shot / Chewed-up Meat / Bus Billboard / Big Fat Face / Spindle-Face / Brawlers / My Mommy); the sole exception is the walnuts -- rewriting to "Winter is coming, and I have been wondering how long you need to store those walnuts" might yield bigger laughs.

## III. Asymmetrical Conflict: A Raisin in the Sun Act I Scene 1

Walter wants Ruth to ask Lena for the entire ten thousand dollars to open a liquor store; Ruth, two months pregnant and considering an abortion, intends to deflect. #1 "Do you know what I was thinking in the bathroom this morning?" -- "No": Inviting her to talk / sneering; #2 Criticizing her bad attitude / lamenting their bitter fate; #3 Insisting she listen / sneering; #4 Ignoring her / mocking him; #5 "When Charlie Atkins wanted me to open a dry cleaner with him, he was just a useless big mouth too, right?" -- Head dropping onto arms: Blaming / concealing guilt (**Foreshadowing**: Her depression makes sense only after the pregnancy is revealed; the actor must not clutch her stomach); #6 Calling her selfish / yielding; #7 Coffee talk about the plan: "Two more sips seem like nothing to you" -- "Bobo?": Enticing her / suspecting a trap (planting sexism to be harvested at the end); #8 Acting like a businessman / foreseeing disaster; #9 Proving worldly wisdom / rejecting nonsense; #10 "Eat your eggs, they are getting cold" -- "A man says 'I am choking, baby' and his woman says your eggs are getting cold!": Placating him / accusing her of offering no support; #11 "Walter, that is not our money" -- Raising the moral hammer / facing defeat (**First Turning Point**); #12 "I am thirty-five, married eight years, and my son sleeps in the living room" -- "Eat your eggs": Begging for sympathy / ignoring; #13 Accusing her of not loving him / accusing him of living in a fantasy; #14 "That is exactly what is wrong with colored women in this world" -- "Some colored men have actually accomplished something": Blaming failure on her / rebuking his failure (**Second Turning Point**); #15 Clinging to a rotten excuse / mocking his self-deception; #16 Soothing wounded ego / shrinking back into fear. Gerunds demonstrate asymmetry: inviting, insisting, blaming, enticing, proving, accusing vs. ignoring, hiding, yielding, placating, retreating. **Three Ways to Ruin a Masterpiece**: Opening with the turning point; delaying the turning point excessively with redundant filler; omitting the turning point altogether.

## IV. Indirect Conflict: The Great Gatsby Chapter 1 Dinner

#1 "You must know Gatsby" -- "Gatsby? What Gatsby?": Baker reveals / Daisy conceals surprise (triggering the book's Inciting Incident); #2 Walking toward the dinner table / Daisy planning to humiliate Tom (letting Nick and Baker carry news of the "troubled marriage" to Gatsby); #3 Snuffing out the candle: Destroying her husband's romantic posture / Tom concealing annoyance; #4 Asking and answering her own question about the summer solstice: Initiating a topic / redirecting the topic back onto herself; #5 "Look, I hurt it," showing her little finger: Getting everyone to look / centering herself; #6 "It is all your fault, Tom": Accusing / hiding behind silence; #7 "That is what you get for marrying a brute of a man" -- "I do not like the word 'hulking'": Openly insulting / ordering her to stop insulting; #8 "A great, big, hulking brute": Attacking again / retreating into silence. Out of 123 words, 107 are monosyllabic, with no character using metaphor; figurative language is reserved for description. #4 and #5 appear not to advance the action, yet serve the spine: a narcissist bending every topic back to herself -- "Gatsby's obsession with Daisy, and Daisy's obsession with Daisy."

## V. Reflexive Conflict: Fräulein Else and The Museum of Innocence

- *Fräulein Else*: "This hotel is huge. Like a strange, dark, magical castle. Everything is so gigantic. The mountains too. Frighteningly huge. They have never been this dark. The moon is not out yet. It will only come out when there is a performance... Oh, Fräulein Else, why are you making such a fuss? You were already prepared to become the mistress of strange men, one after another. Yet you fear the tiny request Herr von Dorsday makes of you? You would sell yourself for a pearl necklace, pretty dresses, and a villa by the sea; is your father's life worth less than those?" -- The first seven sentences average 4.1 words (projected terror); once the critical self takes command, the next seven sentences average 14.5 words; "Damned if you do / damned if you do not": hypocrite -> whore -> coward -> whore -> ingrate.
- *The Museum of Innocence*: The lecturer Kemal recounts to visitors the romantic Kemal of the past; 325 words across 11 sentences averaging 29.5 words, with subordinate clauses forming mini-climaxes; the repetition of "would" adds softness and uncertainty; quoting the romantic Kemal only once: "Today, she, is, coming, now."

## VI. Micro-Conflict: Lost in Translation Initial Bar Conversation

#1 Bob gossiping with the bartender: Exerting charm / pretending to be intrigued (-); #2 Bartender pulling out a stool: Guiding into seat / Charlotte attempting to fit in (-); #3 "Uh... Not sure": Greeting / testing if she is welcome (-); #4 "Suntory time!" -- "Vodka tonic": Putting her at ease / joining in / validating (+); #5 "Taking a break from my wife, forgetting my son's birthday, making two million endorsing whiskey and abandoning stage plays" -- "Oh": Introducing a topic / confessing three failures / hiding surprise (Loss/Gain -); #6 "The good news is the whiskey works," she laughs: Placating / laughing with empathy (+); #7 "My husband is a photographer... I am not doing anything, just tagging along": Inviting her confession / admitting emptiness (-); #8 "How long have you been married?" -- "Two years" -- "Twenty-five long years": Preparing to pursue / parrying / paying court (-); #9 "Did you buy the Porsche yet?": Deflecting pursuit / complimenting wit (+); #10 "Then the marriage drops eight years down to sixteen... There are still car crashes from time to time": Offering hope / complaining / approving (-); #11 "Philosophy" -- "There is good money in that field": Asking for a story / admitting a bleak future (-); #12 "You will figure it out": False hope / laughing it off; #13 "I hope your Porsche plan works out": Offering false hope in return / catching the drift (++ / --); #14 "Cheers": Celebrating self-deception (+++); #15 A long silence: "I wish I could sleep" -- "Me too": Admitting lostness / reciprocating (Loss/Gain --- / Intimacy ++++). Three-tiered progression: bad / worse / worst; loneliness (having something with no one to share it with) vs. lostness (having nothing to share). Charlotte's vocabulary: passive verbs, generic nouns, zero adjectives ("I wish," "I am not sure" twice); Bob's lines resemble any tough guy he has ever played.

## VII. Rewrite Comparisons

- **Blatant Exposition -> Weapon**: "We have known and loved each other for over twenty years, ever since that college fraternity party invited your women's socialist club... my little Trotskyite" -> Have the character hurl the information as a weapon to coerce the other, embed it in an induction scene, or hide the information to keep the behavior believable.
- **On-the-Nose -> Subtext** (*Out of Sight*): "I was afraid you were going to rape me -- Never, all I wanted was a romantic night" -> "Remember when we talked about Faye Dunaway in *Three Days of the Condor*?... Before they went to bed, she accused him of getting rough, and he said, 'What, did I rape you?' She said, 'The night is still young.'"
- **Baring It All -> The Third Thing** (*Sideways*): "I am a difficult man to understand, thin-skinned and bad-tempered... I cannot pursue you, you have to pursue me" -> "Pinot only thrives in very specific little corners of the world, it needs constant care... It will not come looking for you, you have to search for it"; "Please sleep with me" -> "A bottle of wine is like a life, growing and changing until it peaks, just like your 1961, and then enters a steady, inevitable decline. It tastes so good."
- **Mundane -> Imagery** (*Julius Caesar*): "Senator A is so popular and powerful that compared to him, we are all nobodies" -> "Why, man, he doth bestride the narrow world like a Colossus, and we petty men walk under his huge legs and peep about to find ourselves dishonorable graves."
- **Repetitive Beats -> Progression**: "I need to talk to you -- No, stay away from me -- It is important, you have to listen -- Let me go -- You have to listen -- Shut up and go away" (Three pleas / refusals) -> "Just standing there pisses me off -- I am not bothering you, I just want to talk -- I have heard enough -- You haven't heard a single word -- Because I am sick of your bullshit -- It is not bullshit, it is the truth."
- **Subtext Replacing Reaction** (*Terminator 2: Judgment Day*): "Give me your clothes and your motorcycle" -- "Are you crazy, no way" is too shallow -> "You forgot to say please."
- **Parenthetical Additions -> Playing Against the Text** (*Mi Wu Zhui Hun*): "(Angrily, face turning pale) You want the keys? Here, come take them" -> The actor delivers it brightly, haughtily, with innocent charm; an unnatural calm reveals the psychopathy.
- **Small Talk -> Teasing**: "Hi, Debbie" "Hi, Tom" -> "That dress is very sexy!" -- "You like it?" -- "Love it!"
- **Silence** (*The Sopranos*): Carmela takes off the bracelet and necklace, pausing at the ring. Tony merely stares at her. "These are not stolen, right?" He continues staring. She sighs and takes off the ring -- at five cents a word, one could have written a mountain of Tony saying, "I am sorry, the truth is your ring is indeed stolen."

## VIII. Egri: Escalating Dialogue Exemplar

*Ghosts*, Manders vs. Mrs. Alving: "This is all the result of reading bad books" (Attack) -> "My good Pastor, you are mistaken. The one who got me thinking in the first place was you, and I am very grateful to you" (Stronger counterattack) -> "Grateful to me?!" -> "You forced me to submit to duty and obey obligations... once one knot was untied, the whole fabric came undone" -> "Is this the outcome of the hardest struggle of my life?" -> "Say rather it was the greatest defeat of your life" -> "It was my greatest victory, my victory over myself" -> "It was a crime against us both."

*A Doll's House*, Helmer after reading the letter: "No need to put on an act for me (locks the door). Do you understand what you have done?" (Attack) -- "Yes, now I understand completely" (Not a counterattack, but a declaration of war -- the first sign of awakening) -> "Hypocrite... criminal" (Nora says nothing, only staring at him -- silence more expressive than Shakespeare's verse) -> "You have inherited all your father's bad traits" -- "Yes, and this is how I repay you" (Confirming the accusation: she has realized eight years was a nightmare).

## IX. Lu Jun: Dialogue Three Requirements Exemplar

- **Action-Driven Quality** (*Thunderstorm*, forcing the medicine): Zhou Puyuan: "Pour it" -- Fanyi: "I will not drink this bitter stuff" -- "(Loudly to Sifeng) Pour it" -- Zhou Chong: "Father, Mother does not want to, why must you force her?" -- "Neither you nor your mother knows what is wrong with you" -- "(Suddenly stern) Drink it, do not be willful in front of grown children" -- "Chong'er, take the medicine to your mother" -- "Tell your mother to drink" -- Zhou Ping: "Listen to Father" -- "Ping'er, urge your mother to drink it down" -- "Father! I..." -- "Kneel and urge your mother" -- "(Loudly) Kneel!" -- Fanyi (looking at Ping, hastily speaking before he kneels): "I will drink, I will drink now!"
- **Individualized Characterization** (*Blind Date*, two elderly people): "Oh, Old Yan, you sprang this on me with zero mental preparation!" "Stop daydreaming! Prepare for what? Give me a straight answer: yes or no, take it or leave it!" "What is the rush?" "You are sweating already, take a stand!" "You have been bossed around by parents since childhood, and now that you are old, you flip right over to being bossed by your daughter! When will you ever take charge of your own life and call the shots?" "Well, that is just my lot in life!" "You and your fatalism, it will never do!"
- **Subtext** (*Grandmother*): "Kurt, why did you ask me if the story happened like that?" "I just wanted to make sure, Grandma." "You just wanted to make sure, but you must have had a reason to ask." "I was just curious." "Curiosity, but why are you curious?"... (Silence, the young man picks up a book, the grandmother continues watching him) "Kurt, you have to tell me right now what this is really about..."

## X. Lu Jun: Lyric Writing Exemplars

- **Setting the Rhyme by Character Personality**: Li Yutao: "Hahaha! She is back, she is back, oh yes, she is back, Captain's lady Li Yutao! Husband snagged a thirty-sixth-rank sesame-seed post; count it on your fingers, not too big, not too small." Trotter Mother: "Born with a warm heart, started matchmaking at eighteen, building bridges, pairing lovebirds, wandering from village to door with pure delight... Fed herself plump as a pig trotter, and Trotter Mother's name rings far and wide." Xiao Ai: "Everyone says I have good fortune, husband does design on an engineering crew... Joy of family is always joy, and reuniting under one roof is pure bliss."
- **Setting the Rhyme on Key Lines** (*Bamboo Garden Melodies*): Mother of Brother Chun: "That single word 'cruel' stirs a belly of grievance and a chest of fire... I waited and waited till the winter plum danced away in the wind, waited till the peach blossoms blushed and dropped into the river, waited till the new lotus rose pure and high, waited till the autumn chrysanthemums yellowed and still none belonged to me... For whom did I bleed and weep for twenty years? For whom did I taste sourness and bitterness for twenty years? For whom did I suffer shame and insult for twenty years? For whom did I choke down fury and hate for twenty years? In the end, all I earned was an empty dream, graying temples, grudges wide as three rivers, cold stares from all four corners, playing the servant before eyes and behind backs -- and with one word, 'cruel,' you brush it all away in the wind!"
- **Countdown Rhyme on Difficult Endings** (*One Night of Life and Death Love*): Lai A'mao: "One head dull as wood, two eyes searching for faults in others, three shifts mean grueling labor, four limbs sturdy yet anemic, five grains hopelessly confused, six relations severed in utter loneliness, seven feet of man with little passion, eight directions invite nothing but sneers and scolding, ninety-yuan income on an empty bank, truly nine out of ten people shake their heads on sight." Bai E: "Since perfection in ten things exists nowhere on earth, ninety yuan is enough to satisfy me; eight-sided polish may be fine, yet seven feet of man values standing firm; six relations severed can be renewed, five grains confused can be learned from scratch; four limbs sturdy are robust and strong, three shifts still need someone to work them; two hearts willing are all that matters, so strike the gavel and give your answer."
- **Monorhyme** (*Beneath the Pomegranate Skirt*): Dr. Hong: "Forgive this old man for deceiving you; deceiving you was truly protecting you; had I indulged you back then, Wenhui's death would have ruined you... The lash of conscience tortures you, the whip of morality flogs you, the shadow on your soul envelops you, leaving you without peace for a lifetime; tell me, should I have stopped you?" Housemaid Wu: "The wedding banquet was eaten, the wedding candies were handed out, the gratuities were pocketed, the wedding rites were completed, the guests have all left, the hour is late, the Second Young Master should return, and the bride wants to sleep."
- **Sentence Structure Variations** (*Peach Garden Melodies*, Carrying the Sedan): Taohua: "Is that peach tree diseased, broken, wounded, or dead?" Liu: "Every branch of this treasure is green, every leaf tender, every root thick, every trunk robust." "Could it be a hen with sores --" "What do you mean?" "Rot beneath the feathers --" "Heaven forbid"... Chorus: "Lifting that sedan chair with eyes brimming with tears, one foot already stepping beside the coffin."
- **Palindrome** (*The Nüzhen Convent*): "Hating passionate longing as spring goes by, spring once gone leaves longing turned to hate. Gloomy without purpose, I wrong another; wronged by another, my heart bears no gloom. Truth turns false and false turns true; grace breeds grievance, and grievance breeds grace."
