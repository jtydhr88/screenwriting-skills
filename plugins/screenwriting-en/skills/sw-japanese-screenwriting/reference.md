## 1. Kakehi Masaya, *Can of Beauty*, First Draft Plot Concept (2005.01.17)

Yuta, a 23-year-old freeter, is going to an interview at a supermarket tomorrow; his cohabiting girlfriend Haruko wraps herself in a quilt, holds him tightly, and weeps before leaving on a business trip, promising to visit the zoo together when she returns; as he sees her off, a middle-aged man in sportswear named Tomioka moves in next door, helped by several young and attractive women (**Introduction: Encountering the beauties**); Yuta forgets his resume for the interview; at the supermarket, he sees Tomioka again buying massive quantities of groceries and women's lingerie, accompanied by four or five beauties; late at night next door, there is a bingo party and the sound of gasping, driving Yuta's curiosity higher and higher; in the morning, an army of over thirty beauties greet him together like pampered pets; taking advantage of Tomioka's absence, Yuta sneaks inside, discovers a pink "Can of Beauty," and steals one; it includes a DVD titled "How to Make a Can of Beauty," and after thirty minutes in the bathtub, "Saki" is born (**Plot Point: Life with the beauty begins**); Saki possesses transplanted memories; that night, Yuta's sexual advances are rebuffed and Saki flees in fright, falling asleep sitting in front of the supermarket; he apologizes and they walk home holding hands, "Saki is an utterly ordinary woman"; breakfast tastes terrible; she has classes to attend, "She is a college student (premise)--discovering details about her premise little by little"; the DVD main menu contains "Saki's Premise," and halfway through playing it she returns; on the third day, a zoo date, "I always feel humans are very selfish"; that night, he discovers printed on her waist: "Best Before 05.04.10" (**Turn: Accelerating the story with a time limit**); for three days he dares not touch her; on a rainy night he goes for another interview, while Saki watches the premise DVD alone and falls into a daze; Yuta returns, finds the DVD has been watched, and dashes out; Saki stands drenched in the rain outside the supermarket, and Yuta walks up beside her unable to utter a word; at this moment, Haruko returns from her business trip and looks at the calendar--today is 05.04.08, retrieves a "can in blue packaging" from the kitchen, and recalls the night before her trip--on the exposed waist of Yuta, there was also printed: "Best Before 05.04.08!"; outside the supermarket in the rain, Saki's eyes reflect Yuta, the ticking countdown of a clock audible. The End.

**Method Note**: After devising the sudden major twist that "the man is also an android," trace backward to plant setups and add an exposition at the beginning that "the theater youth's dream was shattered" to expand the plot--"this is precisely how a feature film ought to be constructed"; the theater youth was deduced backward from the premise (you need someone who never suspects and can be swept along repeatedly; an actor carries the shadow of a puppet); beauties "belong neither to men nor to women" -> androids; when adapting into a 20-minute episode of *Tales of the Unusual*, cut the breakup storyline, swap in a freeter youth, change the ending to a rainy sea (more cruel), and trace backward to add a seaside date.

## 2. Kakehi Masaya, *Loss:Time:Life* (Simultaneous Pair Episode), Plot Concept

Mori Yasuho, a 27-year-old rookie manga artist; Yasuko, a 27-year-old office worker (married, dissatisfied with her marriage). Yasuko bought the magazine publishing Yasuho's manga and bought a gift; Yasuho rescheduled his manuscript submission to the morning; on the train, a pair of elementary school kids playing mobile games triggers memories (going with Yasuko to buy *Dragon Quest*)--at 9:15 AM the train derails, everything around freezes, soccer referees appear before them as injury stoppage time begins, the electronic board reads "3 Hours," and live commentary remarks, "A youth chasing his dreams seems to have rarely wasted his life" (**Introduction**); down the tracks in a residential neighborhood, countless referees in yellow uniforms sprint over; he calls Yasuko, but the call fails; visiting Yasuko's parents' home, her mother reveals Yasuko married last year; Yasuko comes running up--behind her follow four referees as well (**Elaboration**: having delayed fifteen minutes to deliver the gift, she boarded the very same train); chatting on park swings, visiting their alma mater, "I only got married because you were so oblivious, Yasuho"; flashbacks: returning a love letter with typos corrected, brushing shoulders in middle school hallways, hiding in the high school parking lot; the gift is a wristwatch, its glass shattered and frozen at "9:15" (**Final setup**); Yasuho has "15 minutes" left, Yasuko has "1 hour 15 minutes"--a discrepancy in added time (**Turn**); the two return to the train, Yasuho with "1 minute" left; Yasuko suddenly remembers: "Let me deliver that manga to the publisher!" "A manga artist whose breakout work is his posthumous work is way too cool," snatching the manuscript away by force; "Yasuko... I've loved you all along!" "You're saying it way too late!" The whistle blows; Yasuko sprints desperately toward the publisher, notices typos in several dialogue balloons, corrects them one by one with pencil and eraser, reading through tears; the editor mistakes her for Yasuho's wife--"I was his longtime assistant," she leaves with a smile. The End.

**Method Note**: "What if there were stoppage time right before dying, AND it were broadcast with live soccer-style commentary"--"what if" plus "and"; inspired by a painting (a bullet crawling forward) and the notion that "a person sees slow motion right before dying"; "a man and a woman dying simultaneously in the very same accident without realizing it until later" serves as the "Answer Movie" to *Can of Beauty*; the finished work relocated the "Elaboration" into the second half as an agonizing sudden twist.

## 3. Kakehi Masaya, *Renai-Beya* (Love Room Layout), Concept Key Points

"What if rooms were swapped, AND the lover who appears in the new life turns out to be the previous tenant's partner"; Yui (an art college student aiming to become a chair designer--to share a common language with the architect Takashi) and Atsuko (a lighting designer) swap apartments; professional wrestler moving crew (real material encountered simultaneously by chance across newspapers and magazines); setup props: men's boxer shorts on the floor, masked wrestler charm on phone straps (identical on both), a mug bought by Takashi, a ticket stub; the trigger for discovering the swap: watching a live TV broadcast of a fire crowd, Atsuko spots Yui walking into Room 202 where she used to live; the ending was decided from the start as "moving once again, and then beginning a new life once again." Producer's theme: "Francfranc 15th anniversary, Tokyo, furniture" -> moving scenes naturally showcase furniture.

## 4. Miki Satoshi, *Instant Swamp*, Working Files (Tree Software)

- **Rough Outline 1** (41 numbered micro-materials): An apparently ordinary woman reveals an unexpected side -> kleptomania? / Life gradually sinks into a slump / Debts keep mounting / Finds a cute nail that nobody appreciates / Should she join a new religion? / Believes only what she sees with her own eyes / Collecting 27 ten-yen coins from Showa 53 will cause something to happen / Only sells a single tiny beckoning cat all day / Resigns / Begins running an antique shop / Many things going poorly stems from the swamp's curse--she once threw a friend's belongings into the swamp, reaping retribution / Easily duped yet tells lies / On the verge of a dead end, receives help from a suspicious old man (actually a good person) / A kappa appears / Rescues the stuffed doll thrown into the swamp / Obtains one million yen / The old man wants to redeem the village storehouse / Soil gets purchased / But the soil is not soil; it is the dried swamp -> how should this reveal unfold? Direct exposition is too boring--chocolate milk / The swamp was formerly called the Master's Swamp, sealed away by that very storehouse--that's it! An instant swamp! / A dragon ascends to the heavens, clearing the sky / Two things merge into one.
- **Plot Structure 1** (Finished draft elements): My life always runs into misfortune, blamed on a curse; my morning begins with a glass of chocolate milk; brewing espresso with instant coffee; resigning; antique shop struggles; the old man appears; fermentation and misfortune share the same Japanese pronunciation; constantly searching for ten-yen coins from her birth year; telling punk rockers, "You guys are great, but you haven't nailed your song order!"; storehouse filled entirely with dirt, disappointment; a dragon takes flight from the swamp into the sky; you are terrible at holding an umbrella; a bizarre gadget that meows like a cat when pressed; the Black Cat Shop selling only black antiques; the old man's reason for becoming a swindler; claiming to unseal the seal is nothing more than popping open a brand-new can of Milo; turn on the faucet!
- **Opening Monologue** (Excerpt): "Today is chocolate milk again. ... Running, running, the protagonist of an indie film runs without stopping; people who say 'life is just about running' are rare in reality. ... 1 spoonful of happiness and 10 spoonfuls of Milo plus 35cc of milk, crying is joyful too. Stir it up and you have chocolate milk. ... Whatever happens or doesn't happen out there in society, my morning always starts with chocolate milk."
- **Script Draft 2 vs. Script Draft 9 Ending**: Draft 9 added Haname's speech atop an aerial work platform: "Listen to me, nothing in this world is that big a deal, we laugh far longer than we cry, we witness things we never believed in, but after a night's sleep we forget every bit of it. No matter what! Turn on the faucet! Let it wash away your lies, your greed, and your vanity!"--"It laid bare the parts of myself I found embarrassing."
- **Structure**: "Temporary family" (someone enters a completely different environment in order to grow, then returns); a person who denies the illusory ultimately encounters the bizarre; the partner "Gas" was crafted out of physical contrast.

## 5. Kosawa Ryota, *Gonzo: The Legendary Cop*, Episode 1 Framework

At the very top, "Kuroki's Dream" (inner melancholy and psychological trauma) -> his current persona forced to return to desk duty at the police precinct -> female lead Tsuru (rookie police officer) appears -> a friend holding the key to the case loses a violin -> "Yomogi" is the culprit of a certain case, later renamed "Nicholas"--"What is written within the framework is organized in the exact order in which it must be presented to the audience"; the dead center divides into three parts presenting scenes with three cross-cutting shots. Protagonist Kuroki: once an illustrious figure in the First Investigation Division, now suffering from mental illness; lead actor Uchino Seiyo requested "more agility" -> contrast between a playful exterior and inner disorientation. Producer's reaction to the first draft of *Gaiji Keisatsu*: "The content is overly accessible... it does not matter if people cannot follow it"--taking on the challenge of a "distorted script."

## 6. Omiya Ellie, *Manager Kinoshita and Me*, Project Proposal (2009.10.14)

1. **Synopsis**: Major advertising agency Marumaru Tsushin operates on performance-first, efficiency-first principles; newly appointed department manager Kinoshita Konosuke regularly arrives late, leaves early, and "clocks in just to coast"; how did he become manager? Department members react in wildly varied ways (opposition/indifference/resignation/requesting transfers); under an annual salary system pegged to department sales, they have no choice but to rally; taking seemingly whimsical actions as catalysts, Kinoshita unexpectedly pulls off tasks with finesse, and even his failures yield greater benefits for the company; pitted against him is the Kimijima Department (the elite), colluding with Human Resources and Internal Audit to stage repeated sabotage--the offensive-defensive clash between these polar-opposite teams forms the dramatic highlight.
2. **Characters**: Manager Kinoshita (Itao Itsuji)--catchphrases: "Time to head home," "Either one works," "Whatever is fine"; personal motto: "It's nothing, nobody died." Boku (Ikeda Kazuma)--coolly observes and analyzes the workplace through the lens of modern youth to deliver reports for the audience; constantly at loggerheads with peer Aikawa Masaya.
3. **Synopses for the First Two Episodes + 12 Episode Titles**.
4. **Project Purpose and Highlights**: Not a standard corporate drama, but "an accessible humanistic drama that resonates with everyone navigating society"; each Kinoshita Department member's distinct humor "instantly heightens reality and strikes straight at the heart"; "what truly matters is uncovering the essence of things"; nostalgic like *Terauchi Kantaro's Family*, laced with pure literary flavor inside a humanistic comedy like *I Am a Cat*.

**The Omiya Method**: Clarify the client's commission requirements -> define your own theme ("Computers are friends" / "Forward-looking hiatus" / "Cheering up salarymen") -> establish tone and onomatopoeia -> arrange characters like planting a flowerbed or prepping hot-pot ingredients -> dialogue springs from internal monologue; "The characters move inside my head; all I need to do is write them down"; "If you can write words, you can write a screenplay"; when crew members say something is impossible, reframe the question: "How can we make it work?" (shooting a 70-minute continuous take over two days).

## 7. Fukuda Yuichi, *33-Minute Detective*, Fixed Formula

A case occurs -> Inspector Otahara arrives at the detective agency (there is always a bizarre visitor talking to Rikako) -> everyone rushes to the scene, and Kurama Rokuro announces, "Let me use a full 33 minutes to crack this case" -> captures the initial suspect and delivers an absurd, illogical deduction -> **relocation shots** (the Rainbow Bridge carried like a portable shrine, questioned by no one) -> "I just can't believe someone would kill so casually." Straight-man distribution: in draft one Otahara was the straight man while Rikako was the airhead -> producer's wife: "Isn't that backward?" -> Otahara has absolute faith in the detective, serving as the "grand funny man," while Rikako cuts in: "That's not how it is." Sourced from two ingredients: "XX-man is cooler because he can defeat his opponent in 30 minutes" + Tsuka Kohei's *The Atami Murder Case*.

## 8. Yukisada Isao, *Luxurious Bone*, Prose-Poem Plot Concept

"Three goldfish are kept in a blender; three people peer at the goldfish--two women and one man--exchanging inscrutable glances with hidden secrets between them... sometimes the three sleep huddled together, and one woman stands up, staring motionless at the other two sleeping faces, then switches on the blender containing the goldfish"--Nagase Masatoshi: "This is brilliantly written!"; 10 million yen budget: 8 million for production, 2 million for marketing; founded the company Mouchette. Real-life origin: a newlywed friend kept goldfish in a blender, "Who did he go with back then again...?"

## 9. Takahashi Izumi, *Morning Bath*, Two Versions of the Final Scene

- **Initial Version**: Kitagawa rides a bicycle to work amidst blooming cherry blossoms while Shizu sees him off: "Remember to bring it, riding a bike is fast!" "I'll try, that's a deal, bye-bye"--he still wears the beaded prayer bracelet on his wrist (unclear whether he truly broke free from the religion).
- **Finished Work**: On the morning of their farewell, Shizu utters "stranger" as her final line, the sound of her chewing pickled vegetables echoing as the stage fades to black--"Only by fully acknowledging each other as 'others' and parting ways can both step onto the next rung," "Writing this ending brought tears to my eyes."
- Method: Hand character premise sheets ("Suddenly a religious story begins," "Going to a seminar") to actors for improvisation, drafting the screenplay from their on-set reactions, yet over 90% of dialogue is crafted by the screenwriter; the clash surrounding the bankbook is "real" (Namiki collapsed from hyperventilation).

## 10. Yokohama Satoko, *Bare Essence of Life*, Two Initial Plots

"Yojin is showered in pesticides, dies, is reborn, dies again, and finally has his brain devoured by a bear" / "Machiko is a kindergarten teacher who meets Yojin and falls in love; watching Yojin transform after the pesticide shower, she lives with him, and ultimately ventures into the forest with the kindergarten children"--braiding both plots together and bridging the gaps between them was "extraordinarily arduous." Keywords: children and animals (living without purpose), evolution, ADHD, pesticides (drugs only alter the brain while the world around stays unchanged), headless people, "God," encountering a bear placed in the final shot. Dialogue principles: preserve ambiguity, prioritize acoustic presence, speak only of what actually occurred.

## 11. Uchida Kenji's Toolkit

- **Resume**: Number of siblings, middle school, high school, and university attended, reason for moving to Tokyo and duration of stay, hobbies, attitude toward women.
- **Diary**: Write a three-day diary from the protagonist's POV covering three days of story time -> "So that was what he was thinking back then" -> deduce setups backward (deceiving the other party before escaping -> plant the prop earlier).
- **Framework**: Four frameworks plus concept notes -> itemize shots and dialogue line by line -> revise repeatedly.
- **Timetable**: For multi-character, single-day stories, draw a timeline to the left of names to enforce physical consistency (taking a train from here to there takes more time).
- **"Un-aesthetic" Coincidence**: A phone battery dying is un-aesthetic; a phone vanishing earlier due to an accident is aesthetic; audiences will not tolerate coincidence at crucial junctures.
- **Returning to What You Love Most**: "Please make the adult video that excites you most"--do not pivot to shooting what everyone else likes; an original screenplay has no need for scenes you detest (strip clubs).

## 12. Arai Haruhiko, *W's Tragedy*, Adaptation Key Points

The original novel's snowbound lodge locked-room murder was "too dull to work with"; Kadokawa Haruki: "Change whatever you like as long as you keep the title"; after a month, conceived treating the original novel as a play-within-a-play framing the theme of an "understudy chasing her dream"; interviewed girls in theater training workshops for a month; referenced *All About Eve* and *Sunset Boulevard*; W = first initial of original protagonist / WOMEN / double V; at the press conference, the girl willingly plays scapegoat in exchange for the lead role--"If I hadn't taken money from him, you'd all believe we were genuinely in love, wouldn't you?"; "She is lying, but her tears are real"; creative process: one month scouting and interviews, one month on synopsis, setting it aside to drink and play, then polishing the entire script over the final two weeks before deadline. *This Country's Sky*: "If you write eating, you must write what dishes were eaten; if you write lovemaking, write everything from undressing to the finish"--"daily life embedded inside extraordinary events."
